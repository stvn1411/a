What is the High Cholesterol program and how is it meant to work?

Cholesterol is a fatty substance in the blood. When LDL (“bad”) cholesterol is high, it can build up in artery walls over time, which is why people look for ways to bring it down. Many turn first to food, which is where this program sits.

The program is published by Blue Heron Health News and takes what its page calls an oxidized-cholesterol approach: it says that cutting out one hidden ingredient in everyday food is the key step. The details are inside the paid guide, so we could not check them. What we can say is that the format is a digital PDF with lifetime access and free updates, with no equipment, drugs or treatments to buy.

It is worth reading with your doctor in mind, because blood tests are the only reliable way to see how your cholesterol is changing.

Does a cholesterol diet program like this help?

No trial has tested this program, so we looked at what research says about diet and cholesterol in general:

  • The American Heart Association's advisory reviews trials in which replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated vegetable oil reduced cardiovascular disease by about 30%, similar to the reduction from statin treatment. It concludes that swapping saturated for unsaturated fats lowers LDL cholesterol and the risk of heart disease.[1]
  • The same advisory notes that replacing saturated fat with refined carbohydrates and sugars did not lower heart disease rates, so what you swap in matters.[1]
  • The 2018 US cholesterol guideline is the reference doctors use to decide when diet changes are enough and when medicine is also needed.[2] It is worth asking your doctor where you stand.

What this means for you: food is a proven lever for cholesterol, and a clear guide can make the changes easier to start and stick with. Use the program together with your doctor's plan, keep taking prescribed medicine, and retest your cholesterol to see what is working.

How to get the most out of the High Cholesterol program

  1. Get a baseline. Ask your doctor for a cholesterol blood test before you start.
  2. Make changes gradually so they last, rather than overhauling everything at once.
  3. Swap, don't just remove. Replace saturated fat with unsaturated fat such as olive oil, nuts and fish.[1]
  4. Keep taking prescribed medicine unless your doctor tells you to change it.
  5. Retest as your doctor advises, to see which changes are helping.

Is the High Cholesterol program safe? Things to check

A diet guide is low risk for most people, but high cholesterol is a medical issue linked to heart disease and stroke, so it deserves proper medical follow-up.

  • Do not stop cholesterol medicine because of anything you read. Your doctor can tell you when diet alone is enough.[2]
  • Check supplements, herbs and big diet changes with your doctor, especially if you have diabetes, kidney disease or take regular medicine.
  • Call emergency services for chest pain, breathlessness, weakness on one side or trouble speaking.
  • The program is general information, not individual medical advice.

How the High Cholesterol program compares with other options
