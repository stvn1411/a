What is Men's Cooch Ball Program and how is it meant to work?

The Men's Cooch Ball Program pairs a small, soft ball with a guided plan. You sit on the ball, breathe calmly and follow short sessions that aim to release tension in the muscles and connective tissue around the pelvic floor, then build strength with exercises in the later weeks.

The pelvic floor is a group of muscles that supports the bladder and bowel and plays a part in sexual function. Research has tested pelvic floor muscle training in men with erection difficulties, with encouraging results.[1][2] The seller's own explanation, that the ball improves blood flow and releases fascia, is the company's framing; what the studies tested was pelvic floor exercise.

The plan follows an acronym, P.E.L.V.I.C., across six weeks: prepare with posture and breathing, energize with micro-movements, lighten pressure, build vitality, integrate release with strengthening, and keep results going.

Do pelvic floor programs like the Men's Cooch Ball Program work?

The research on pelvic floor exercise for men is encouraging:

  • In a randomized trial of 55 men with erection difficulties, those who did pelvic floor exercises with biofeedback had a mean increase of 6.74 points in the erectile function score at 3 months compared with men given lifestyle advice only, and results kept improving at 6 months.[1]
  • In a randomized trial of men with erection difficulties at least a year after prostate surgery, three months of pelvic floor muscle training led to significantly better erectile function than no training, and the effect was maintained at follow-up.[2]
  • These studies used exercise programs, some with biofeedback equipment, and did not test the Cooch Ball. The program's final weeks combine the ball with pelvic floor exercises, which is the closest match.

What this means for you: pelvic floor training is a research-supported place to start, and a guided plan with a ball makes it easy to build the habit. The ball itself has not had its own clinical trial, so give the six weeks a fair go and use the guarantee to judge for yourself.

How to get the most out of Men's Cooch Ball Program

  1. Start gently. Use low pressure and short sessions, and build up to the three minutes a day the seller suggests.
  2. Let your breath be the guide. The seller's one rule is to stay calm and keep breathing; if your breath turns erratic or you hold it, get off the ball.
  3. Follow the weekly plan, using the five-minute quickstart each week.
  4. Add the strength exercises in weeks 5 and 6, which is where the research on pelvic floor training is closest.
  5. Be consistent. Trials of pelvic floor training typically measured results after about 3 months.

Is Men's Cooch Ball Program safe? Side effects

The seller describes the ball as gentle when used as directed. As with any exercise program, listen to your body and start slowly.

  • The seller advises waiting until you have healed from recent pelvic or prostate surgery, and checking with a healthcare provider if you have a diagnosed pelvic floor condition, severe pain that has not been evaluated or an active infection.
  • Stop if you feel sharp or shooting pain; mild soreness can occur if you overdo it.
  • Do not use the ball over open wounds or infections in the pelvic area.
  • See a doctor for persistent pain, blood in urine, trouble urinating or new erection problems, which need a professional check.
