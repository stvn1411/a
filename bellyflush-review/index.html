What is BellyFlush and how is it meant to work?

BellyFlush is a herbal capsule aimed at people who want help with regularity and the heavy, sluggish feeling that goes with it. Eleven plant ingredients are combined in each two-capsule serving: herbs traditionally used to help the bowels move, herbs traditionally used to soothe the digestive lining, and a purified clay.

The approach is easy to understand. Senna, cascara sagrada and cape aloe are classic stimulant-laxative plants, and senna in particular is one of the most widely used over-the-counter options for occasional constipation. Marshmallow root and slippery elm are soft, mucilage-rich herbs often used for comfort, while fennel is a traditional digestive herb. Triphala is a three-fruit blend from Ayurvedic tradition.

The sales page does not list the amount of each herb, so we have judged the formula on its ingredients and the research on them. Because several of the herbs are active laxative plants, it fits best as an occasional routine rather than something to take indefinitely.

Does BellyFlush work?

BellyFlush has not published a clinical trial of its own, but its key ingredient class is well understood:

  • An evidence review of over-the-counter constipation treatments rated senna as grade A for efficacy, alongside polyethylene glycol.[3]
  • A critical review of 43 papers on stimulant laxatives found no strong evidence that they damage the gut, which is reassuring for occasional use.[4]
  • A systematic review of laxatives in long-term care found senna was as effective as, or better than, the alternatives studied, although the authors called long-term evidence inconclusive.[5]
  • A purified clay similar to bentonite helped people with constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome in a trial of 524 people.[2]

What this means for you: the herbs that do the heavy lifting in BellyFlush are among the best-supported over-the-counter options for occasional constipation, and the soothing herbs are a pleasant companion. The finished formula has not had its own trial and the doses are not listed, so use it as directed for short periods and keep up fibre, water and movement.

How to get the most out of BellyFlush

  1. Follow the label amount and no more. If you are new to herbal laxatives, read the bottle first and do not exceed the stated serving.
  2. Take it with a large glass of water, as the official directions say. The sales page gives two timings (before bed, or with a meal); follow the bottle label for yours.
  3. Use it in cycles. Herbal laxatives are meant for occasional use; if you need help most days, see a doctor to find out why.
  4. Support it with food: fibre-rich foods, fermented foods and regular meals. The free e-books are a handy starting point.
  5. Keep hydrated and stay active. Water and a daily walk make a noticeable difference to regularity.

Is BellyFlush safe? Side effects

Senna and cascara are used for occasional constipation by many adults. Like any stimulant-laxative herb, they can cause cramping, loose stools or urgency, especially at higher amounts, and they are not meant for long-term daily use.

  • If you are pregnant, nursing, taking prescription medicine (including heart medicines or diuretics) or have a medical condition, check with your doctor first.
  • Licorice root can raise blood pressure at higher amounts; people with high blood pressure should ask a doctor.
  • Stop use and see a doctor if you have severe or persistent abdominal pain, blood in the stool, vomiting, or diarrhea that lasts.
  • Dietary supplements are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[1]
