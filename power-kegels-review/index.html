What is Power Kegels and how is it meant to work?

Power Kegels is a digital program that teaches men to train the pelvic floor, the group of muscles that sits between the legs and supports the bladder, bowel and sexual function. The sales page leads with a short, five-second technique it promotes as a way to last longer in bed.

Pelvic floor exercises, often called kegels, are well known for helping with bladder control, and urologists have also studied them for erectile function and ejaculation control. The idea is simple: the same muscles that help create and maintain an erection can be strengthened with practice, just like any other muscle.

Because Power Kegels is a digital program, there is nothing to take and nothing to ship: you buy it online and use the members area. The lessons are delivered by video, so we describe what the official page states and the research behind the approach, rather than the individual lessons.

Do pelvic floor exercises like Power Kegels work?

The research on pelvic floor training is among the better evidence available for a non-drug approach in this area:

  • A randomized controlled trial of 55 men with erectile dysfunction compared pelvic floor exercises with biofeedback plus lifestyle changes against lifestyle changes alone. Erectile function was significantly better in the exercise group after 3 months.[1]
  • At 6 months, 40% of the men in the exercise group had regained normal erectile function and 35.5% had improved, according to the trial's authors.[1]
  • A later study of pelvic-floor muscle rehabilitation reported benefit in erectile dysfunction, though effects on ejaculation were harder to assess.[2]
  • The evidence for lasting longer in bed is earlier-stage, and the trials used supervised programs rather than a self-guided course.[3]

What this means for you: pelvic floor training is a research-backed, low-risk habit, and Power Kegels makes it easy to start at home. The program itself has not had its own clinical trial, so give it several weeks of consistent practice before judging.

How to get the most out of Power Kegels

  1. Start gently and build up gradually, as with any new muscle training.
  2. Practice daily. The research measured results after 3 to 6 months of regular exercise.
  3. Learn to find the right muscle (the one you would use to stop the flow of urine) and avoid tensing your abdomen, thighs or buttocks.
  4. Breathe normally during each contraction and release fully between repetitions.
  5. Support it with the basics: regular exercise, good sleep, a balanced diet and not smoking, all of which support sexual health.

Is Power Kegels safe? Side effects

Pelvic floor exercises are widely considered low risk when done as directed, and the program contains no ingredients. A few sensible precautions apply.

  • Overdoing the exercises can cause muscle tiredness or tension, so build up gradually and rest if anything hurts.
  • If you have pelvic pain, recent pelvic or prostate surgery, or a urinary condition, check with your doctor first.
  • See a doctor for ongoing erection problems, pain or urinary symptoms; these deserve a proper check.[1]
