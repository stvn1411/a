What is Eat Stop Eat and how is it meant to work?

Eat Stop Eat is a book that explains one specific way to do intermittent fasting. The idea is simple: on one or two days a week you go without eating for a pre-set stretch of time, and on every other day you eat as you normally would. You never go a whole day without a normal meal, and there are no foods you have to give up.

The author, Brad Pilon, MSc, built the method from his own review of the fasting research, and the book walks you through the reasoning, how to start, how to eat around your fasts and how to adapt the plan for women, for muscle and for keeping weight off. The current 5th edition comes with a Quick Start Guide for people who want to begin straight away.

It works by making it easier to eat a little less overall, which is the one thing every weight-loss approach has in common, without the daily effort of a conventional diet.

Does Eat Stop Eat work? What the research shows

Fasting-based eating has been studied in a number of trials, and the findings are encouraging for anyone who finds ordinary dieting hard:

  • A meta-analysis of 15 randomized trials with 758 adults with overweight or obesity found that intermittent fasting reduced body weight by about 3.7 kg on average.[2]
  • An umbrella review of meta-analyses reported moderate reductions in body weight, body mass index and several cardiometabolic risk factors with modified alternate-day fasting.[3]
  • In a one-year randomized trial of adults with obesity, alternate-day fasting did not outperform daily calorie restriction, which means you can choose the style you find easier to stick with.[1]

What this means for you: fasting is a research-supported way to lose weight, and Eat Stop Eat packages one simple version of it with plenty of practical guidance. The studies used a range of schedules rather than this exact one, so treat it as a sensible approach to try for a couple of months and judge by how you feel and what the scale and tape measure show.

How to get the most out of Eat Stop Eat

  1. Start with the Quick Start Guide and pick a fasting day that is naturally busy, so the hours pass easily.
  2. Begin with one fasting stretch a week and only add a second if the first feels comfortable.
  3. Drink water, tea or coffee during your fast and eat a normal, satisfying meal when it ends.
  4. Eat well on other days. The book's own suggestion is a diet of mostly lean protein, vegetables and fruit, though you can use any style you like.
  5. Add some strength training to help keep muscle while you lose weight, and track progress over weeks rather than days.

Is Eat Stop Eat safe? Side effects

Eat Stop Eat asks you to go without food for a set period on a couple of days a week. Many healthy adults manage this well, but it is not right for everyone, and the official terms say so plainly.

  • The official terms say people who are pregnant, nursing, diabetic or have a history of eating disorders should not follow the program.
  • Ask your doctor first if you take medicine for blood pressure or blood sugar, have a medical condition, or are under 18.
  • Mild hunger, tiredness or headache can happen at first. If you feel dizzy, faint or unwell, eat and speak to a doctor.
  • Stop and speak to a professional if fasting begins to feel stressful or leads to unhealthy thoughts about food.
