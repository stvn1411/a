What is Reviv and how is it meant to work?

Reviv is a soft appliance made of silicone that sits over the teeth while you sleep. After you mould it, the seller says it feels snug but barely noticeable and does not interfere with breathing. It comes in two models, Reviv 1 and Reviv 2, each in more than one size.

The seller markets Reviv as a way to support jaw alignment and posture. Its explanation, which compares the soft tissue of the face to a balloon, is the seller's own theory rather than established science, and we do not repeat the larger claims about aging here. What we can say is that this is a plain, simple product: a piece of food-grade silicone, a sizing guide and a night-time routine.

The seller also says about 8,000 people have used the appliance and method since early 2023. That is the seller's own figure and we could not independently verify it, but it suggests a product that is in real use.

Does an appliance like Reviv work?

This is the part of the review where we are most careful, because there are no published clinical trials of Reviv itself. What research exists is about oral appliances in general:

  • A Cochrane-style systematic review of 37 randomized trials looked at oral splints for jaw disorders and tooth grinding. It rated the evidence as very low certainty and found no clear pain benefit across all jaw disorders combined.[1]
  • The same review found no evidence of adverse events linked to splints, although it noted that harms were poorly reported.[1]
  • The testimonials on Reviv's order page are personal accounts from users and are not clinical evidence. Some reviewers say they noticed changes after a few weeks, while one describes the effect as possibly placebo.
  • Splints in those trials are a different type of device from Reviv, so the findings do not directly apply.

What this means for you: Reviv is a simple, low-risk-looking product to try if the idea appeals to you, but the research does not yet show what it does. Think of it as a comfortable night-time routine, check with your dentist first and judge the result by how you feel.

How to get the most out of Reviv

  1. Read the sizing guide first for your model and double-check the size before you order.
  2. Talk to your dentist before you start, especially if you have jaw symptoms, braces or recent dental work.
  3. Follow the seller's moulding instructions that come with the appliance so it fits snugly.
  4. Keep it clean by rinsing it after use and storing it dry, as you would with any mouth appliance.
  5. Stop and ask a professional if you notice jaw pain, soreness or bite changes. Also see our Healosen review if you want to log sleep and habits alongside your routine.

Is Reviv safe? What to watch for

Silicone oral appliances are generally comfortable for most people, and the systematic review we cited found no evidence of harm from splints, though it noted harms were poorly reported. Some precautions still apply.

  • Check with your dentist first if you have jaw pain, clicking, a bite problem, braces, retainers or recent dental work.
  • If you have sleep apnea or any breathing problem, ask a doctor before wearing anything in your mouth at night.
  • Stop using it and see a professional if you notice jaw soreness, tooth pain or a change in how your teeth meet.
  • Keep it away from young children and pets, as with any small mouth product.
  • Statements about this product on the seller's page have not been reviewed by us or the FDA.

How Reviv compares with other options

  • Dentist-made night guards: custom-fitted by a professional and the usual choice for diagnosed teeth grinding or jaw disorders.
  • Over-the-counter boil-and-bite guards: similar in concept and often cheaper, though they differ in material and fit.
  • Professional dental or orthodontic care: the right route for bite or alignment concerns. Reviv is a simpler, self-directed option.
