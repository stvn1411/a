What is The Sciatica Recovery System and how is it meant to work?

The Sciatica Recovery System is a digital course created by Matt Cook, who says he lived with sciatica for twelve years before learning a slow, attention-led style of movement. The course turns that style into a step-by-step set of video lessons.

The central idea is what the author calls Body Consciousness: instead of stretching hard or counting repetitions, you move very slowly and notice what each muscle, tendon and joint is doing. The course begins with three guided Body Consciousness flows (seated, standing and lying down) so you can choose the one that suits your comfort level that day.

From there it works through the areas people commonly associate with sciatic discomfort: the foot, ankle and calf, the knee, the hip joint, the piriformis and buttock area, and the lower spine. Sessions are short, and everything is delivered online, so there is nothing to ship and you can start the same day.

Does gentle exercise help with sciatica?

The idea at the heart of this course, keeping the body gently moving rather than resting for long periods, is consistent with what researchers have looked at:

  • A systematic review and meta-analysis asked how effective physiotherapy is for people with clinically diagnosed sciatica, including exercise, manual therapy and education, using randomized trials with a control group.[1]
  • In a single-blind randomized trial of 181 people with severe sciatica, those given symptom-guided exercise improved significantly more than those given sham exercise on most outcomes.[3]
  • A 2025 network meta-analysis of chronic sciatica found that exercise combined with neural mobilization was among the options with a favorable short-term result for leg pain, while the authors noted the overall evidence is still uncertain and no single approach has been proven best.[2]

What this means for you: gentle, guided movement is a sensible and well-supported part of looking after sciatica, and this course makes it easy to start. The course itself has not been through a clinical trial, so treat it as a helpful routine alongside, not instead of, advice from your own doctor or physiotherapist.

How to get the most out of The Sciatica Recovery System

  1. Start with a Body Consciousness flow. The course puts this first for a reason: it sets the slow, attentive pace for everything else.
  2. Move slowly and stay within comfort. A gentle stretching feeling is fine; sharp pain is a signal to ease off.
  3. Be consistent. The author suggests about 15 minutes, three times a week, and short sessions make that realistic.
  4. Pair it with everyday movement such as short walks and breaks from long sitting, as your doctor advises.
  5. Keep a simple note of how sitting, walking and sleeping feel from week to week.

Is The Sciatica Recovery System safe? What to know first

The movements are described as gentle and slow, and the course is delivered digitally, so there are no ingredients or devices involved. As with any exercise, a little muscle awareness or tiredness is normal; sharp or increasing pain is not.

  • The author recommends showing the exercises to a medical professional first if you have any reservations.
  • See a doctor promptly for new or worsening leg weakness, numbness in the groin or inner thighs, or loss of bladder or bowel control. These need urgent care.
  • Check with your doctor first if you are pregnant, have had back surgery or have a diagnosed disc or nerve condition.
