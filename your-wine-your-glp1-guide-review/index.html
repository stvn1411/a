What is Your Wine + Your GLP1 Guide and how is it meant to work?

Your Wine + Your GLP1 Guide is a short digital guide for people taking GLP-1 medicines, such as semaglutide or tirzepatide, who still want to enjoy a glass of wine. It is written in a warm, humorous voice by Alexandra, the founder of Wine & Drama, who describes her own journey on these medicines and says plainly that she is not a doctor, nutritionist or coach.

The guide's starting point is that a blanket 'just do not drink' is not very helpful. Instead, it offers a plan: what to eat before wine, how to pace yourself, what to do the next day, and when not to weigh yourself. It also covers the common trap of 'starting over on Monday'.

The sales page notes that your tolerance can change on these medicines, which matches the growing research interest in how GLP-1 medicines affect drinking.[1]

What does research say about GLP-1 medicines and alcohol?

There is no clinical trial of this guide, so we looked at the research behind its central idea, that GLP-1 medicines can change how drinking feels:

  • In a randomized, double-blind phase 2 trial of 48 adults with alcohol use disorder, low-dose weekly semaglutide reduced the amount of alcohol people drank in a lab test compared with placebo.[1]
  • The same trial found that semaglutide significantly reduced drinks per drinking day and weekly alcohol craving, although it did not change average drinks per calendar day or the number of drinking days.[1]
  • This was a small trial in people with alcohol use disorder, not in people on weight-loss doses who drink socially, so it shows that GLP-1 medicines can change the experience of drinking rather than proving how any one person will react.[1]

What this means for you: there is genuine scientific interest in how GLP-1 medicines affect drinking, which supports the idea of having a plan before you pour. The guide offers practical habits, not medical advice, so use it alongside the guidance of your prescriber.

Is Your Wine + Your GLP1 Guide safe? What to know

The guide is general lifestyle information, not medical advice, and the author says so herself. Alcohol affects everyone differently, and this is especially important to remember on GLP-1 medicines, where tolerance can change.[1]

  • Ask your prescriber whether alcohol is appropriate for you, especially if you have diabetes, liver problems or take other medicines.
  • If you have had problems with alcohol in the past, or are in recovery, seek professional support rather than a lifestyle guide.
  • Do not use this guide to change your dose, medicine or injection schedule.
  • If you feel unwell after drinking, such as persistent vomiting, severe abdominal pain or dizziness, seek medical care.
