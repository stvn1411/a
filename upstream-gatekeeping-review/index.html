What is Upstream Gatekeeping and how is it meant to work?

The book is titled I Broke Free from Pornography, and So Can You. We have named this review after its central method, Upstream Gatekeeping, to keep the page clean and easy to find. It is written by Elijah Sparks, who says he struggled with pornography for forty years, with serious effects on his marriage, family and reputation, before changing his approach.

His core idea uses a river picture. For years he tried to use his strongest self-control when temptation was already at its peak, like standing in the rapids. He now argues that the battle may begin earlier, at a calmer moment when a thought appears and attention lingers, and that learning to recognise that upstream moment makes the choice easier to exercise. The book explains how he found it and how he applies it.

It is positioned as a personal account and a practical method rather than a clinical programme, and it is notably free of shame. The author says more shame was not the answer for him, and that he treats a setback as information about what happened before it. Do the ideas in Upstream Gatekeeping hold up?

The book does not claim clinical proof, and the research we found supports its building blocks rather than the method itself:

  • Wanting and liking are different. Researchers describe how repeated use of a rewarding behaviour can amplify cue-triggered wanting without necessarily increasing enjoyment, which matches the book's point about why a decision made earlier can feel weak in the moment.[1]
  • Noticing triggers early is a recognised skill. A pilot randomized trial of mindfulness-based relapse prevention in adults leaving substance-use treatment found it feasible, with initial signs of benefit compared with usual care. It concerned substances rather than pornography, so it is a related idea, not direct proof.[4]
  • Habit change takes patience. The average was about 66 days, with a range from 18 days to far longer, so slow progress with setbacks is normal.[3]

What this means for you: Upstream Gatekeeping is a thoughtful, practical way to think about timing, and it fits with established ideas about reward, habits and early awareness. It has not been tested as a method, so treat it as one useful tool, and consider professional support if the pattern is affecting your life.

How to get the most out of Upstream Gatekeeping

  1. Read it through once without judging yourself. The author's own advice is that more shame is not helpful.
  2. Think about your own upstream moment. Notice when a thought, a time of day or a feeling such as boredom, tiredness or loneliness usually comes first.
  3. Make a small plan for that moment, such as putting the phone down, changing rooms or contacting someone you trust.
  4. After a setback, ask what happened before it. The book treats this as information you can use next time.
  5. Look after the basics. Sleep, stress and loneliness often sit behind urges. A steady evening routine helps, and our Health Protocols review covers one.
  6. Add support. A trusted friend, a support group or a counsellor can make progress easier.
