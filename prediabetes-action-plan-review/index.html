What is The Prediabetes Action Plan and how is it meant to work?

Prediabetes means blood sugar is higher than usual but not yet in the diabetes range. It often shows up on routine lab work, and many people are not sure what to do next. This guide is meant to fill that gap with plain-English explanations and a clear, ordered plan.

The author, Dr. Yaw A. Boateng, presents the book as a translation of current American Diabetes Association and CDC guidance into a protocol you can follow. It focuses on improving insulin sensitivity through the tools with the most published support: food composition, portioning, movement, sleep and stress.

It is general health education, not individual medical advice, and it is designed to be used alongside your own doctor's guidance. Nothing here is presented as a cure.

Does a plan like this work?

The guide itself has not been tested in a clinical trial, but its core approach, a lifestyle program built around food, activity and gradual weight loss, is one of the best-studied in the field:

  • In the Diabetes Prevention Program, 3,234 adults at high risk were randomly assigned to placebo, metformin or a lifestyle program aiming for at least 7% weight loss and 150 minutes of activity a week. Over an average of 2.8 years, the lifestyle program lowered the rate of new type 2 diabetes by 58% compared with placebo, and was more effective than metformin.[1]
  • A 10-year follow-up of the same participants looked at how long these effects lasted. The lifestyle group lost weight and then partly regained it, a reminder that habits you can keep matter more than quick fixes.[2]

What this means for you: the research supports the kind of habits this guide teaches. The guide is a structured way to start and keep them going, and it works best alongside testing and advice from your own doctor. Results vary with your starting point and how consistently you follow the plan.

How to get the most out of the guide

  1. Get your numbers first. Know your latest fasting glucose and A1C, and note them in the guide.
  2. Read chapters 1 to 6 once through, then start with the Meal Plan so your next meal is already decided.
  3. Use the 30-Day Action Calendar to add one small habit at a time rather than changing everything at once.
  4. Print the Grocery List and keep the Restaurant Guide on your phone.
  5. Share the plan with your doctor and recheck your labs on the schedule they recommend.

Is it safe? Who should check first

A food-and-movement guide is low risk for most adults, but blood sugar is a medical topic and your situation is personal.

  • If you take glucose-lowering medication or insulin, are pregnant or manage another significant condition, review any major diet or activity changes with your healthcare provider first.
  • If you have symptoms such as extreme thirst, frequent urination, blurred vision or unexplained weight loss, see a doctor rather than relying on a guide.
  • Increase activity gradually, especially if you have been inactive or have heart or joint concerns.
