What is Body Reset Sound Formula and how is it meant to work?

Although it is listed in the weight-loss category, Body Reset Sound Formula is not a diet, a workout plan or a supplement. The official page says so plainly: it is a collection of guided "healing songs" that combine soothing music with spoken, supportive words, delivered as MP3 files you keep.

The creator, Joanne Hartwell, explains the idea this way: stress, self-criticism and old beliefs about food and body image can sit behind habits like late-night snacking, so a few quiet minutes of guided listening each day may help you feel calmer and more in control around food. She says the songs draw on techniques from her therapy practice, namely guided relaxation, visualization and positive affirmations.

Each of the six songs has its own theme, from body shame to emotional eating and self-compassion. Listening takes about 7 minutes, so it fits easily into a morning or an evening routine. The program is meant to sit alongside, not replace, healthy eating, movement and medical advice.

Does guided audio like Body Reset Sound Formula work?

Body Reset Sound Formula itself has not had its own clinical trial, so we looked at the research on the kinds of tools it uses, namely guided relaxation, hypnosis-style audio, guided imagery and music:

  • In the HYPNODIET randomized trial, 80 adults with obesity and high food impulsivity who had hypnosis sessions plus self-hypnosis training had much lower disinhibited eating scores after 8 months than a group with nutrition education alone. The sessions were led in person, but the results are encouraging for calm, guided approaches to eating behavior.[3]
  • In a small randomized wait-list trial, women with overweight or obesity who practiced guided imagery daily for 35 days showed reductions in food cravings compared with the wait-list group, and the study reported 89% compliance with the daily practice.[4]
  • A 3-week pilot of an audio self-hypnosis file found no significant weight difference against a placebo audio file, though the authors noted the study was small and recommended larger trials. That suggests the audio is best seen as a support for habits and stress rather than a quick route to weight loss.[5]
  • A meta-analysis of 14 experiments with 706 healthy adults found that the effect of music on stress recovery was not statistically significant overall, but results varied with the type of music and who chose it, so finding audio you personally enjoy matters.[2]

What this means for you: the research suggests that calm, guided listening can support eating behavior and cravings, especially as part of a wider routine, while audio on its own is not a proven weight-loss method. Think of Body Reset Sound Formula as a relaxing daily habit that may make healthy choices feel easier, and pair it with balanced meals and movement. Results vary from person to person.

How to get the most out of Body Reset Sound Formula

  1. Start with Song 1 and read the 12-page manual so you know what each song is for.
  2. Pick a regular time. The creator suggests listening in the morning before getting out of bed, but any quiet time works.
  3. Use headphones in a safe, comfortable place, and never while driving.
  4. Keep the Craving Crusher handy on your phone for moments when a craving or stressful urge hits.
  5. Use the journal to note feelings that come up, and go at your own pace with the deeper songs (5 and 6).
  6. Keep up the basics: balanced meals, regular movement, good sleep and check-ups. Guided audio works best on top of these.

Is Body Reset Sound Formula safe? Side effects

There is nothing to swallow, so there are no ingredient side effects or supplement interactions to worry about. The main thing to know is that some themes are emotional, and some people feel reflective, tearful or tired after a session. That is usually a normal response to relaxation and reflection.

  • If you have an eating disorder, or are being treated for trauma, anxiety or depression, check with your doctor or therapist before starting.
  • The official page notes that the songs do not treat any medical condition, so keep working with your doctor on conditions like thyroid problems, PCOS or menopause symptoms.
  • The page's own disclaimer says its statements have not been evaluated by the FDA and that weight loss is typically achieved with a healthy reduced-calorie diet and exercise.[1]
  • Never listen while driving or operating machinery, since the songs are meant to relax you.
  • If difficult feelings come up and do not settle, speak with a qualified professional.
