What is Booster Brew and how is it meant to work?

Booster Brew is a powder you stir into a drink each morning. Instead of one headline ingredient, it combines six groups of nutrients that are each linked to circulation, energy or male vitality, so you get the whole routine in one glass.

The central idea is nitric oxide, a molecule your body uses to relax and widen blood vessels. L-citrulline and L-arginine are amino acids that feed this pathway, and beet root is a well-known source of dietary nitrate that does something similar. Epimedium, Panax ginseng and tongkat ali are traditional herbs that have been studied in men's sexual health, and a probiotic blend rounds out the formula.

The sales page also describes the tunica albuginea, a fibrous tissue in the penis, as the reason for choosing these nutrients. That is the manufacturer's own framing; we focus here on what the individual ingredients are known for.

Does Booster Brew work?

We could not find a clinical trial on Booster Brew itself, but its main ingredients have real research behind them:

  • In a pilot study of 24 men with mild erectile dysfunction, 12 improved from mild to normal erection hardness on L-citrulline compared with 2 on placebo, with no adverse events reported.[3]
  • In a randomized, double-blind trial of 119 men, ginseng berry extract improved total sexual function scores over 8 weeks, and blood hormone and lipid markers stayed unchanged.[2]
  • A meta-analysis of randomized trials found that beetroot juice and dietary nitrate lowered systolic blood pressure, a sign of healthier blood vessel function.[4]
  • For tongkat ali, the pooled result across two trials was not significant overall, but men with lower baseline scores improved, and the authors say more trials are warranted.[1]

What this means for you: Booster Brew brings together several ingredients with encouraging, if modest, human research. The studies used particular extracts and doses, so results from the finished drink may differ. Give it the 6 weeks the company suggests, and keep up the basics: sleep, exercise and a balanced diet.

Is Booster Brew safe? Side effects

The ingredients are common ones, but the company does not publish a side-effect list. As with most amino acid and herbal formulas, some men may notice mild digestive upset, and combining blood-vessel-relaxing ingredients with certain medicines can matter.

  • If you take blood pressure or heart medicine, nitrate medicine or erectile dysfunction medicine, check with your doctor before starting.
  • If you have a medical condition, are taking any medicine, or are pregnant or nursing, the manufacturer itself advises consulting your physician first.
  • Dietary supplements aren't reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[5]
  • See a doctor for ongoing performance problems, chest pain or dizziness. Changes in sexual function can sometimes point to other health issues that deserve a proper check.
