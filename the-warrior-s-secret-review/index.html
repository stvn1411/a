What is The Warrior's Secret and how is it meant to work?

The Warrior's Secret is a digital course rather than a supplement. According to the official page, it teaches you how to prepare a fermented food mix using three ordinary ingredients from a grocery store, inspired by a historical story about a Korean general's pre-battle meals.

The course arrives as a PDF, audio and video files, and the page says you can use PayPal or major cards. After the first couple of weeks the author suggests a smaller maintenance amount.

The page is written in a dramatic style, with strong claims. We focus here on the practical side: it is a food-based self-help course, not a medical treatment, and it is a low-risk habit for most healthy adults.

Does the approach behind The Warrior's Secret work?

  • In a randomized trial of 110 obese men aged 35 to 55, weight loss and more activity were associated with better sexual function in about one third of men with erectile problems at the start.[1]
  • A meta-analysis of 7 studies and 478 participants found that exercise improved erectile function scores.[2]
  • A randomized trial in 55 men compared pelvic floor exercises with biofeedback plus lifestyle changes against lifestyle advice alone, and the authors concluded the exercises are worth considering as a first-line approach.[3]

What this means for you: eating better is a research-supported habit, and the course makes that concrete with a recipe. We could not verify the page's specific claims about the recipe, including comparisons with prescription medicine, so we do not repeat them. Use the 60-day guarantee to judge it for yourself.

Is The Warrior's Secret safe? What to know first

Eating more whole and fermented foods is low-risk for most healthy adults. A few points are worth knowing first:

  • Erection or ejaculation changes can have medical causes, including heart and blood-vessel conditions, diabetes, hormone issues, medicines and stress. A doctor's check is a sensible first step, and it is quick and private.
  • If you take prescription medicine, including ED or blood-pressure medicine, do not stop or change it because of a guide. Talk to your doctor first.
  • Fermented foods can bother some people's digestion, and people with a weakened immune system or on certain medicines should ask a doctor first. The course is not a substitute for medicine you already take.
  • See a doctor promptly for chest pain, shortness of breath, an erection that lasts more than four hours, pain, or a sudden change in sexual function.

How The Warrior's Secret compares with other options

  • A doctor visit: a clinician can look for medical causes and discuss proven treatments. This is a sensible first step alongside any course.
  • Prescription medicines: they work for many men but need a prescription. The course does not replace them, and you should not stop medicine without your doctor.
