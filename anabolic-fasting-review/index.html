What is Anabolic Fasting and how is it meant to work?

Anabolic Fasting is a digital program rather than a pill or powder. You get a manual that explains what to eat, when to eat and how to train, plus workout plans and a set of video MasterClasses that explain the reasoning behind each step.

The idea starts from a well-known problem: when people diet, they often lose muscle along with fat, and less muscle makes it harder to keep the weight off. Brad Pilon, who wrote Eat Stop Eat, designed the protocol to tackle that problem by combining a specific fast with a large protein intake and short resistance workouts, so the body gets a strong signal to hold on to muscle.

The official page says the protocol was inspired by research from Maastricht University on how the body uses protein after exercise. It does not name the paper, but the closest match we found is a 2023 study from that university, which we describe below.

Does the Anabolic Fasting approach work?

The parts that make up the protocol each have solid research behind them, and the combination is a sensible one:

  • The 2023 Maastricht University study found that 100 g of protein produced a bigger and longer-lasting (more than 12 hours) muscle-building response than 25 g after exercise. This is the closest match we found to the research the program mentions.[1] It measured muscle protein building in a lab, not fat loss or body shape over time.
  • A 12-week trial of 16:8 time-restricted eating found that eating only within an eight-hour window did not beat normal eating for weight loss, and a small group measured in person showed a slight drop in lean mass.[3] That is exactly the gap a plan with built-in protein and strength training aims to close.
  • A one-year trial found alternate-day fasting and daily calorie restriction led to similar weight loss, so a fasting-based plan is a fair option if it fits your lifestyle. More people dropped out of the fasting group, which is why a plan designed to make fasting easier is a good idea.[4]
  • Across 49 trials, adding protein to resistance training increased muscle size and strength, with benefits levelling off at around 1.6 g/kg a day.[2]

What this means for you: the research behind each piece, protein, resistance training and fasting, is real and encouraging. What the program adds is a clear way to combine them. The finished protocol has not been tested in its own published trial, so think of it as a well-built plan based on sound principles rather than a proven result.

How to get the most out of Anabolic Fasting

  1. Read the manual first and watch the MasterClasses before you start, so the schedule makes sense.
  2. Plan your protein. Research suggests intakes above about 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day add little extra muscle, so aim for a steady, realistic amount.
  3. Do the workouts. Start with the twice-a-week plan and move to the three-day plan only if you recover well.
  4. Stay hydrated and keep sleep regular, especially on fasting days.
  5. Track more than the scale: waist measurements, strength in the gym and how your clothes fit often move before weight does.
  6. Use the email access if you hit a plateau or need to adjust the plan for your schedule.

Is Anabolic Fasting safe? Who should check first

Fasting and strength training are safe for many healthy adults, but they are not right for everyone. The official page itself advises talking to a physician first if you are pregnant, nursing, taking medication or have a medical condition.

  • If you have diabetes or take glucose-lowering medicine, blood pressure medicine or any other prescription, check with your doctor before fasting.
  • Anyone with a history of disordered eating, who is pregnant or nursing, underweight or under 18 should not follow a fasting plan without medical advice.
  • Start strength training gradually, use good form and stop if you feel sharp pain, dizziness or faintness.
  • Mild hunger, tiredness and muscle soreness are common when you begin a new routine; see a doctor if symptoms are severe or do not settle.
