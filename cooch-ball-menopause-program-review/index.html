What is The Cooch Ball Menopause Program and how is it meant to work?

The Cooch Ball is a small, soft ball you sit on for a few minutes a day. The idea is to apply gentle pressure to the pelvic floor, encourage blood flow and help you notice and release tension in an area many women never think about. The Menopause Program pairs the ball with a 6-week guided plan called the P.E.L.V.I.C. Formula.

The plan moves week by week: prepare your body with posture and breathing, energize the muscles with small movements, lighten pressure, build vitality as a habit, combine release with gentle strengthening, and then keep the results going. Everything is self-paced, and you can ask questions through the seller's support team, which passes them to the founder, Jana Danielson.

This is a wellness and fitness product, not a medical treatment. The program is about building awareness and strength, not about diagnosing or treating a condition.

Does pelvic floor training like this work?

The research on pelvic floor training in postmenopausal women is encouraging:

  • A systematic review of randomized trials concluded that pelvic floor muscle exercise is a highly recommended intervention for urinary incontinence in postmenopausal women, and that women who receive regular supervision are more likely to keep going and see less leakage.[1]
  • In a randomized trial of 40 postmenopausal women, Pilates and pelvic floor muscle training performed comparably for stress urinary incontinence, provided the pelvic floor was contracted on purpose.[2]
  • A trial of 49 postmenopausal women found that training, with or without biofeedback, was associated with stronger pelvic floor muscles and better quality of life.[3]

These studies tested exercise programs, not the Cooch Ball, and they focused on leakage rather than the full range of benefits on the sales page. Wider claims, such as relief from back pain, sciatica or improved intimacy, are the seller's own and are not supported by the studies we found.

What this means for you: pelvic floor training is a well-supported habit for women after menopause, and this program makes it simple, guided and low-effort. The Cooch Ball itself has not been tested in a published trial, so think of it as a friendly way to stay consistent rather than a proven device.

How to get the most out of The Cooch Ball Menopause Program

  1. Start at the beginning. Week one is about posture and breathing, and it sets up the rest of the plan.
  2. Let your breath be your guide. The seller's one rule is to stay calmly breathing; if your breath becomes erratic or you hold it, come off the ball.
  3. Start short and build up towards about 3 minutes a day. Two to three sessions a week still help, according to the seller.
  4. Consider a pelvic floor physiotherapist. The founder recommends an annual check, especially before starting.
  5. Track your progress through sleep, comfort, leakage and confidence, and note what changes over the 6 weeks.

The program is designed to be gentle, and the seller says an overdone session usually means mild, temporary soreness. Stop if you feel sharp or shooting pain.
