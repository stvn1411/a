What is SharpEar and how is it meant to work?

SharpEar is a herbal capsule sold for ear and hearing support. Its makers describe it as helping with hearing, a calm nervous system, mood and sleep, and the formula reflects that by pairing botanicals traditionally used for circulation and focus with St. John's wort for mood and L-glutamine, an amino acid.

Because it is a capsule, it is simple to fit into a daily routine: no measuring and no taste. The official page does not state the number of capsules per bottle or give directions, so please follow the label on your bottle.

A supplement like this is not a hearing aid and not a replacement for a hearing test. It is a daily formula some people like to add to a healthy routine, and the 60-day guarantee means you can judge it for yourself.

Does SharpEar work?

Honest answer first: there are no published trials of SharpEar itself, so the question is really about what its ingredients are known for. The encouraging parts:

  • St. John's wort has a solid track record in research on low mood: a Cochrane review of 29 double-blind trials found it more effective than placebo and comparable to standard antidepressants.[3]
  • For one standardised ginkgo extract, a systematic review concluded it is an evidence-based option for tinnitus.[2]
  • Ginkgo, vinpocetine and huperzine-A have all been studied for circulation, focus and memory, which fits the "calm and clear" way the product is positioned.

Where the research is less clear: the Cochrane review of ginkgo for tinnitus did not find proven benefit when ringing in the ears is the main complaint,[1] and the vinpocetine evidence is limited and dated.[4]

What this means for you: SharpEar uses well-known botanicals with real research behind them in areas such as mood, focus and circulation. Evidence for hearing itself is limited, so treat it as a daily support formula, keep up regular hearing checks, and use the 60-day guarantee to judge how it works for you.

How the 60-day money-back guarantee works

Every order is covered by a 60-day money-back guarantee. Here is how to use it if you need to:

  1. The 60 days are counted from the day your order is shipped, and a return posted on day 59 still qualifies even if it arrives a few days later.
  2. Fill in the Return and Refund Form that comes with your order.
  3. Mail the bottles and the form to the return address on the refund page. Return postage is paid by you.
  4. Refunds are processed within a few days of your package arriving, and you will get an email when it is done.

Helpful tip: because the clock starts at shipping, not delivery, put a reminder in your calendar about ten days before day 60 so you have time to post the bottles back if you want to.

Orders are processed by ClickBank, which can also help with any order question. Questions about the product go to the support team through the site's Contact page.

How to get the most out of SharpEar

  1. Follow the label. The website does not give directions, so use the serving instructions printed on your bottle.
  2. Take it at the same time each day so it becomes a habit.
  3. Be consistent and keep a diary of how you feel each week; many herbal studies run 8 to 12 weeks.
  4. Mind the clock: the guarantee starts at shipping, so note the ship date from your tracking email.
  5. Look after your ears: keep headphone volume down, use ear protection in loud places and book a hearing test if you have not had one.
  6. Tell your pharmacist what you take before you start, because St. John's wort can interact with many medicines.

Is SharpEar safe? Side effects

SharpEar is described as free of stimulants and not habit-forming, and herbal formulas are generally well tolerated by healthy adults. A few points are worth knowing before you start:

  • St. John's wort can interact with many medicines, including some antidepressants, blood thinners, oral contraceptives and heart medicines, so speak to your doctor or pharmacist first if you take anything regularly.[6]
  • Vinpocetine: the FDA advises women who are pregnant or could become pregnant to avoid it, so SharpEar is not for them.[5]
  • Dietary supplements are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[7]
  • See a doctor promptly for sudden hearing loss, ringing in one ear only, dizziness or ear pain. These need a professional check rather than a supplement.
