What is CelluCare and how is it meant to work?

CelluCare is a capsule supplement from CelluCare Research, marketed to support healthy blood sugar levels and general vitality. Instead of a single active ingredient, it blends a number of plants that people have traditionally used and that researchers have looked at for glucose balance, circulation and everyday energy.

The idea is straightforward: healthy blood sugar depends mostly on food, movement, sleep and stress, and a formula like this is meant to give your body a little extra support on top. Several of the botanicals, turmeric, gymnema and banaba in particular, have been studied in people, and we summarise what we found below.

The order page lists eight named ingredients and says the capsule contains more than a dozen. The full label and the amount of each ingredient are shown on the bottle rather than the order page, so check them before you buy if you have allergies.

Do ingredients like those in CelluCare work?

The research on the main herbs is encouraging, if modest, and it is worth being clear about what it does and doesn't show:

  • A meta-analysis of eleven placebo-controlled trials found that turmeric and curcumin supplements lowered fasting blood glucose, with the clearest effect in people who started with some degree of raised blood sugar.[3]
  • A pooled analysis of six randomized trials of gymnema found lower fasting blood sugar, along with lower cholesterol and triglycerides, and a small fall in diastolic blood pressure.[2]
  • A review of banaba describes blood sugar effects linked to corosolic acid and reports no adverse effects in controlled human trials.[4]
  • The authors of these papers also point out that studies were small, results varied, and larger, longer trials are needed.[2]

What this means for you: CelluCare brings together several herbs with real human research behind them, in a single capsule. The finished formula has not published its own clinical trial, and the amount of each herb is not listed on the order page, so think of it as a supportive extra on top of food, movement and your doctor's advice rather than a replacement for any of them.

  4. Keep your check-ups. If you monitor your blood sugar, keep doing so and share any changes with your doctor.

Is CelluCare safe? Side effects

CelluCare is made of plant ingredients that are widely sold as supplements, and the banaba review we read reports no adverse effects in controlled human trials. Dietary supplements are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale.[1]

  • Herbs studied for their effect on blood sugar, such as gymnema, banaba and turmeric, may add to the effect of diabetes or blood-sugar-lowering medicine. Check with your doctor before starting.
  • If you take blood pressure or blood-thinning medicine, are pregnant or nursing, or have surgery planned, check with your doctor first.
  • As with many herbal supplements, mild stomach upset is the most common complaint; stop and speak to a professional if you feel unwell.
  • See a doctor for symptoms such as unusual thirst, frequent urination, blurred vision or unexplained weight loss; these need a professional check and a supplement is not a substitute.
