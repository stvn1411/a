What is the Fatty Liver Solution and how is it meant to work?

The Fatty Liver Solution is a digital guide, not a supplement. Nothing is shipped: you download it within minutes of ordering and follow it at home. It is written by Julissa Clay and published by Blue Heron Health News, a long-running publisher of health guides.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is the build-up of extra fat in the liver in people who drink little or no alcohol. Medical guidelines describe lifestyle change, mainly eating patterns, weight management and regular physical activity, as the foundation of care. The program is designed around that idea and organises it into a four-week routine.

According to the official page, it works across three areas: a focused detox phase, a diet built around limiting certain trigger foods while keeping many everyday foods, and a movement plan of short daily routines.

Does a lifestyle program for fatty liver work?

The research on diet and exercise in fatty liver is encouraging, and it is the reason we rate this approach well:

  • In a prospective study of 293 people with biopsy-confirmed NASH who followed lifestyle changes for 52 weeks, larger weight loss went with larger improvements in liver biopsy features. The authors concluded that the extent of weight loss was associated with the level of histologic improvement.[1]
  • In a randomized crossover trial in people with NAFLD, a Mediterranean diet lowered liver fat by 39% on average, compared with 7% on a low-fat, high-carbohydrate diet, and improved insulin sensitivity.[2]
  • In a small randomized trial, 8 weeks of resistance exercise reduced liver fat by about 13% in sedentary adults with NAFLD, with no significant change in body weight.[3]
  • A 2021 meta-analysis of randomized trials found Mediterranean-style eating improved markers linked to fatty liver compared with control diets, although the pooled trials were small.[4]

What this means for you: diet quality and regular movement are well-supported areas for people with fatty liver, and this program packages them into an easy four-week routine. The program itself has not been tested in its own published trial, so think of it as a practical guide to evidence-based habits and keep your doctor involved in monitoring your liver.

The program is made of food choices and exercise rather than pills, which suits most adults. Changing what you eat or how much you move can cause temporary tiredness or digestive changes as your routine adjusts.

  • See a doctor promptly for yellowing of the skin or eyes, persistent abdominal pain or swelling, or unexplained weight loss.
