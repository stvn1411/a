What is FortBite and how is it meant to work?

FortBite is a soft powder that you use in place of, or alongside, your usual toothpaste. You tip a small amount on a wet toothbrush and brush as normal for about two minutes, then rinse and spit.

The manufacturer's idea is that two clays (bentonite and kaolin) can help lift unwanted material from the surface of teeth, while calcium carbonate supports the look of healthy enamel and a blend of herbs and spices adds a fresh, clean taste. The page also describes the formula as designed to help keep gums and breath healthy.

Clay powders have a long history in traditional oral care, and a formula built from simple natural ingredients with a pleasant spiced-mint flavour can make brushing something you look forward to.

Does a clay tooth powder like FortBite work?

Research on clay tooth powders as a finished product is limited, so here is what we can say:

  • The clays are well known in traditional use; a review of around 100 published articles found bentonite has a wide range of actions on the body and recommends further study.[2]
  • A laboratory study with human periodontal cells found no toxic effect from kaolinite's conditioned medium, and no pronounced change in the inflammation markers measured.[3]
  • We did not find a published clinical trial of FortBite itself, so we treat the claims as the manufacturer's view and results as varying from person to person.

What this means for you: FortBite is a simple, pleasant way to add a natural-ingredient powder to the brushing you already do. The finished formula has not had its own clinical trial, so judge it by how your mouth and breath feel after a few weeks of regular use.

How to get the most out of FortBite

  1. Use it twice a day, morning and night, brushing for about two minutes as the page directs.
  2. Use a small amount on a wet brush, then rinse and spit.
  3. Be consistent for several weeks before judging, and notice how your mouth and breath feel.
  4. Keep up the basics: clean between your teeth and keep your regular dental check-ups.
  5. Ask your dentist whether a clay powder suits your teeth and whether to keep a fluoride toothpaste in your routine, as FortBite's page does not list fluoride.

Is FortBite safe? Side effects

FortBite is made from simple mineral and plant ingredients, and the manufacturer reports no notable side effects. We could not independently verify the manufacturer's safety statements, so a few sensible checks are worth making.

  • If you have sensitive teeth, worn enamel or gum problems, ask your dentist whether a clay powder is right for you.
  • Stop using it if you notice irritation, and avoid it if you are allergic to cinnamon, clove, ginger, myrrh or mint.
  • Dietary products are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, and the manufacturer suggests showing the jar to your doctor if you have a medical condition or take medication.[1]
  • See a dentist for pain, bleeding or receding gums, or loose teeth.
