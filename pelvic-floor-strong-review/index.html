Does pelvic floor training like Pelvic Floor Strong work?

The research on pelvic floor training is some of the most encouraging in women's health:

  • A Cochrane review of 31 trials with 1,817 women found that pelvic floor muscle training cured or improved symptoms of stress incontinence in around 74% of women versus 11% in control groups (3 trials, 242 women).[1]
  • For any type of urinary incontinence, 67% of women who trained reported cure or improvement versus 29% of controls (2 trials, 166 women).[1]
  • The review's authors suggested pelvic floor muscle training could be part of first-line conservative management.[1]

What this means for you: a guided routine that builds pelvic floor strength is a well-founded place to start, and Pelvic Floor Strong makes it easy to begin. Those results come from pelvic floor training in general, and the program's own stretch has not been tested separately, so give it a few weeks of regular practice and see how you feel.

How to get the most out of Pelvic Floor Strong

  1. Start with the digital version. You can begin straight away and revisit the videos whenever you like.
  2. Set a regular time for your routine, such as after your morning coffee, so it becomes a habit.
  3. Be consistent. Studies of pelvic floor training usually measure results after several weeks of regular practice.
  4. Go at your own pace and stop if anything is painful.
  5. Note your progress: how many leaks you notice in a week, and how confident you feel when you cough, laugh or exercise.

Is Pelvic Floor Strong safe? Who should check first

The program is gentle exercise, so there are no ingredients or side effects to worry about. As with any exercise program, a little muscle tiredness while you get started is normal.

  • If you are pregnant, recently gave birth or have had pelvic surgery, check with your doctor or a pelvic health physiotherapist before starting.
  • Stop and speak to a professional if you feel pain, heaviness or bulging in the pelvic area.
  • See a doctor for leakage that begins suddenly, is painful, comes with burning, fever or blood in the urine, or does not improve, since these need a proper check.
