What is Migraine and Headache Program and how is it meant to work?

The Migraine and Headache Program is a digital guide from Blue Heron Health News, a publisher of natural-health guides. You buy it once, download it straight away, and follow it at your own pace at home.

The sales page tells the story of a reader who had migraines every 10 to 12 days, followed a trigger-focused program, and then a simple set of migraine exercises. The seller describes the program as built on “medically-validated migraine data”. As with any personal story, results vary and it is not a clinical trial.

The idea is practical: learn what tends to set your migraines off, and add a gentle exercise routine, instead of relying only on medicines. The official pages list references from well-known migraine organisations such as the American Migraine Foundation and the Migraine Trust.

Does the Migraine and Headache Program work?

We could not find a published trial of this specific program, which is common for digital guides. What we can say is that its two main ideas are ones researchers have looked at:

  • Regular aerobic exercise has been tested as migraine prevention in a randomized study with relaxation and topiramate as comparison groups, with the authors suggesting exercise is an option for people who don't want, or don't benefit from, daily medication.[1]
  • Paying attention to personal triggers is a standard part of migraine self-care, and the sales pages cite well-known migraine charities as references.[2]
  • The seller's own case study reports a big improvement, but it is one person's story. The page itself says results may vary.

What this means for you: the program pairs two sensible, research-supported ideas, exercise and trigger awareness, in an inexpensive format with a real refund policy. Give it a few weeks, keep a simple headache diary, and keep your doctor in the loop.

How to get the most out of Migraine and Headache Program

  1. Start with the trigger section and keep a short headache diary for a few weeks: date, food, sleep, stress, weather and how you felt.
  2. Build the exercises into your week rather than doing them only when a migraine is coming.
  3. Go gently. Start slowly and build up, especially if you're new to exercise.
  4. Keep taking prescribed medicine unless your doctor tells you otherwise, and share the diary at your next visit.
  5. Reassess at week 6 to 8. Compare your diary with the weeks before you started.

Is Migraine and Headache Program safe? Who should check first

The program is a self-help guide with no pills or devices. The main safety point is that headaches and migraine can occasionally be a sign of something that needs urgent care.

  • See a doctor promptly for a sudden, severe headache, headache with fever, stiff neck, confusion, weakness, or changes in vision or speech.
  • Check with your doctor before starting new exercise if you are pregnant, have heart or blood-pressure problems, or have another health condition.
  • Don't stop prescribed migraine medicine because of any guide without talking to your doctor.
  • The seller notes that case studies are individual and that you should consult your doctor before acting on health information.

How Migraine and Headache Program compares with other options

  • Prescription and over-the-counter medicines: work quickly for many people and are managed by your doctor. The program is a complement, not a replacement.
  • Other self-help programs and apps: migraine trackers help log patterns. The Migraine and Headache Program adds a structured exercise plan.
  • Professional care: a neurologist or headache clinic can diagnose the type of headache and tailor treatment. Nothing replaces that.
