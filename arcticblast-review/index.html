What is ArcticBlast and how is it meant to work?

ArcticBlast is a liquid you apply in drops. You put a few drops on the sore area, massage them in gently and let the formula do its work. There are no pills to swallow and no creams to scoop out of a tub.

Its leading ingredient is menthol, a classic counterirritant. Menthol makes the skin feel cool and then warm, and that sensation distracts you from the ache underneath. Camphor and wintergreen oil, which is the source of methyl salicylate, are used in much the same way in many familiar pain-relief rubs.[1]

The remaining ingredients, aloe, arnica flower extract, St. John's Wort oil and calendula extract, are traditional botanicals chosen for a soothing, skin-friendly feel.

Do cooling pain-relief drops like ArcticBlast work?

Counterirritant pain relievers are among the most widely used topical options, and the research on their key ingredients is encouraging:

  • In a triple-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of 10 workers with chronic arm and hand pain, menthol gel reduced pain intensity more than a placebo gel, with about 1.2 points more relief on a 0–10 scale.[2]
  • In a randomized, double-blind trial of 208 adults with mild to moderate muscle strain, a methyl salicylate and menthol patch gave about 40% more pain relief over 8 hours than placebo, with similar rates of side effects.[3]
  • In a 21-day trial of 204 people with finger osteoarthritis, an arnica gel eased pain and improved hand function about as well as a 5% ibuprofen gel.[4]

What this means for you: a menthol, camphor and wintergreen formula is a sensible, research-supported way to take the edge off everyday aches. These trials tested other products (a gel and a patch), and ArcticBlast itself hasn't had its own clinical trial, so give it a week or two of regular use to see how it works for you.

How to get the most out of ArcticBlast

  1. Apply a few drops to the sore area and massage in gently. The sales page gives both 3 to 4 drops and 2 to 3 drops, so starting with the smaller amount is sensible.
  2. Use it at least twice a day, in the morning and before bed, or as needed.
  3. Wash your hands afterwards and keep it away from your eyes.
  4. Use it on clean, unbroken skin and stop if any irritation develops.
  5. Pair it with gentle movement and rest as your doctor or physio suggests, and notice how the area feels over the first couple of weeks.

Is ArcticBlast safe? Side effects

Cooling and warming products are generally well tolerated when used as directed. The most common issue is mild skin redness or irritation where they're applied.

  • Check with a doctor first if you have a skin condition, are allergic to any ingredient (including salicylates), or are pregnant or nursing.
  • It is not for anyone under 18. Keep it out of reach of children, and don't use it if the safety seal is damaged or missing.
  • Stop using it and seek medical advice if you have a negative reaction.
  • Statements about this product have not been evaluated by the FDA.[6]
  • See a doctor for severe, sudden or worsening pain, swelling or redness.

How ArcticBlast compares with other options

  • Roll-ons: a roll-on is tidy and easy to reach your back with, while drops give you control over exactly how much you use.
  • Creams and gels: classic rubs do the same counterirritant job; drops are quick to apply and light on the skin.
  • Pills: oral pain relievers act throughout your body, while a topical stays on the sore spot and nothing is swallowed.
