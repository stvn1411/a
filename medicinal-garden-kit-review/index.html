What is Medicinal Garden Kit and how is it meant to work?

The Medicinal Garden Kit is a box of seeds, not a supplement. Inside are ten small packs, one for each herb, plus a guide that explains how to plant, grow and harvest them and how to turn the harvest into teas, tinctures, salves and other traditional preparations.

The herbs are a charming mix: chicory, yarrow, California poppy, marshmallow, chamomile, evening primrose, lavender, echinacea, calendula and feverfew. Several are well-known cottage-garden flowers that attract bees and butterflies, and the maker notes that most are perennials or self-seeding annuals that come back each year.

The sales page is written around self-reliance and traditional plant remedies. We think the kit is best enjoyed as a gardening and learning project, with home-grown herbs used as teas and skin-care preparations in the traditional way, alongside regular medical care.

Does growing your own herbs make sense? What the research says

There is no research on the kit itself, which is simply seeds and a guide. What we can look at is what is known about the herbs, and the honest picture is that it is modest and uneven:

  • An oral lavender oil capsule was found comparable to lorazepam for generalized anxiety in a six-week double-blind trial, though this was a standardized preparation rather than garden lavender.[2]
  • The NIH says echinacea may slightly reduce your chances of catching a cold.[5]
  • For chamomile, the NIH says there isn't yet enough reliable evidence to rate its use for specific conditions.[4]

Where the kit really shines is the experience itself: tending a colourful, fragrant garden, harvesting your own herbs for tea and learning old plant traditions.

What this means for you: treat the kit as a rewarding gardening project and a source of herbal teas and skin-care preparations, rather than a replacement for medicine. The plants are lovely, the guide is included, and for anything that needs treatment, keep seeing your doctor.

How to get the most out of the Medicinal Garden Kit

  1. Read the included guide first. The maker says it covers planting, growing and harvesting for each of the ten herbs.
  2. Start with the easy ones. Calendula, chamomile and California poppy are cheerful, simple flowers to try first.
  3. Plant in a sunny spot and group herbs with similar needs, and check the guide for seasons and spacing.
  4. Harvest and dry the flowers for teas, and keep notes of what works in your garden.
  5. Learn before you use. Look up each herb's safety before drinking or applying it, and ask a professional if you're unsure.

Is Medicinal Garden Kit safe? Side effects

Growing herbs is a gentle hobby, but using plants medicinally needs some care:

  • Several of these herbs, including echinacea, chamomile, calendula, yarrow and feverfew, are related to ragweed and daisies. The NIH notes that people allergic to those plants may react to chamomile and echinacea.[4][5]
  • The NIH also notes that little is known about chamomile in pregnancy, and that it can interact with warfarin.[4]
  • If you take prescription medicine, are pregnant or nursing, or have a medical condition, check with your doctor before using any herbal preparation.
  • Don't use home-grown herbs in place of treatment for a serious condition, and see a professional for persistent symptoms.
  • Dietary and herbal products aren't reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale.[1]
