What is 14 Day Rapid Soup Diet and how is it meant to work?

The 14 Day Rapid Soup Diet is a digital program, not a supplement. On its sales page it appears as the Flat Belly Soup Diet, and this review covers the same program. It gives you a short plan of soup-based meals, with recipes and clear guidance on what to eat for breakfast, lunch and dinner.

The idea is simple. Soup is mostly water and vegetables, so it fills you up for few calories. The program builds its meals around that idea, and adds bonus cookbooks with extra soup recipes, including slow cooker and low-carb options, so you aren't stuck with the same bowl for two weeks.

Because it's a download, there is nothing to ship and you can start the same day. The creator, Derek, describes himself on the sales page as a health and nutrition coach.

Does 14 Day Rapid Soup Diet work?

We couldn't find a clinical trial on the program itself, so we looked at the research on the ideas it's built on. It's encouraging, because the core idea is well studied:

  • In a randomized trial of 60 normal-weight adults, eating a low-calorie soup before a meal reduced total meal calories by about 20% compared with no soup, and the form of the soup (chunky, pureed or broth) made no real difference.[2]
  • In a 12-month trial of 120 overweight adults, both groups lost about 6.5 kg on a reduced-calorie healthy diet, and weight loss was linked to getting more energy from vegetables.[3]
  • In a trial of 70 people who had recently lost weight, a low energy density diet was associated with less weight regain over seven months than a usual diet.[4]

What this means for you: the studies show that soup and vegetable-rich eating help people feel full on fewer calories, which is a sound basis for a plan. The sales page makes bigger promises than the research does, so we'd set a gradual goal, in line with the 1 to 1.5 pounds a week its own fine print mentions, and treat the program as a structure for healthier eating, not a shortcut.

How to get the most out of 14 Day Rapid Soup Diet

  1. Read the plan through first and stock your kitchen before day one, so soup is easy to make.
  2. Cook in batches. Soup keeps well, and having it ready makes the plan easier to follow.
  3. Use the bonus cookbooks to vary flavors so the plan stays enjoyable.
  4. Drink water and add protein if you feel hungry. Check with your doctor before following any low-calorie plan.
  5. Keep the basics in place: regular movement and good sleep do a lot of the work.

Is 14 Day Rapid Soup Diet safe? Side effects

A soup-based plan is food, not a supplement, so there are no capsules or stimulants to think about. The main points are practical: any low-calorie plan can leave you tired or hungry at first, and some soups are high in sodium, so reading labels or cooking with less salt helps.

  • If you are pregnant or nursing, have diabetes or kidney problems, take prescription medicine, or have a medical condition, check with your doctor before starting.
  • If you have a history of disordered eating, skip rapid plans and talk to a professional first.
  • Aim for a gradual pace. Strong promises of very fast loss on a sales page are not what the research supports.
  • Dietary advice on this page isn't a substitute for medical care, and supplements aren't reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale.[1]
  • Stop and see a doctor if you feel dizzy, faint, or unwell.
