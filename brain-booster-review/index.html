Does Brain Booster work? What the research shows

Brain Booster itself has not been tested in a clinical trial, but the idea that movement and healthy habits support the brain has good research behind it:

  • In a randomized trial of 120 older adults, aerobic exercise training increased the size of the anterior hippocampus and improved spatial memory.[3]
  • The FINGER trial, published in The Lancet, tested a two-year program of diet, exercise, cognitive training and vascular monitoring in at-risk older adults, and similar programs are now being studied worldwide.[2]
  • Researchers still debate how large the effects of exercise on memory are, and Brain Booster's own exercises have not been studied.
  • The publisher says the program has helped more than eight thousand readers; this is the publisher's figure, and results vary.

What this means for you: regular movement and healthy daily habits are among the best-supported ways to look after your brain, and Brain Booster offers a gentle, seated way to start. It is a low-cost companion to a healthy lifestyle and regular check-ups, not a replacement for medical advice.

How to get the most out of Brain Booster

  1. Start gently, and follow the program's steps in order.
  2. Be consistent. Short, regular practice usually beats occasional long sessions.
  3. Pair it with the basics: daily walking, good sleep, a balanced diet, staying socially and mentally active.
  4. Keep up regular check-ups with your doctor, including blood pressure and hearing and vision checks.
  5. Note how you feel over a few weeks, such as energy, focus and mood, so you can decide before day 60.

Is Brain Booster safe? Side effects and cautions

Brain Booster is a program of simple exercises and habits, so it carries few risks for most adults, but exercise should always suit your own health.

  • If you have a heart condition, blood pressure problems, balance issues or are recovering from an injury, check with your doctor before starting any new exercise.
  • Memory and thinking changes can have many causes. Please see a doctor for new or worsening memory loss, confusion or personality changes; these need a professional check and no guide can replace one.
  • If a guide suggests supplements, remember that they are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale.[1]
