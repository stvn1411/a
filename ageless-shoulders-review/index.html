What is Ageless Shoulders and how is it meant to work?

Ageless Shoulders is an exercise program, not a pill or a cream. You get a digital course with step-by-step movements you can start straight away, and a 1 lb Indian club that is mailed to you. The routine is built around gentle swinging and circling movements with the club.

Indian clubs are an old training tool: pairs of weighted clubs were swung in circles for fitness in the 19th century, a history the American Journal of Public Health has written about.[3] Modern versions, often called clubbells, are used to train shoulder rotation, which is the movement of the arm turning in and out at the shoulder joint.

The sales page presents the routine as a way to loosen up and strengthen the shoulders without drugs or surgery, in short sessions that don't need a gym or special space. It is aimed at everyone from active adults to older beginners, with the stated idea that smooth, controlled movement is more helpful than forceful training.

Does a routine like Ageless Shoulders work?

Ageless Shoulders hasn't been tested in its own clinical trial, but the ideas it rests on have real support:

  • A British Journal of Sports Medicine systematic review of 64 randomized trials on subacromial shoulder pain concluded that exercise therapy should be the first-line treatment for improving pain, function and range of motion.[1]
  • In a small randomized trial of 21 healthy male cricket fast bowlers, six weeks of Indian clubbell exercises added to normal training improved internal and external rotation range of motion in both shoulders and improved internal rotator strength, while the training-only group did not show the same gains.[2]
  • The picture is not one-sided: a 2026 meta-analysis of 19 randomized trials (1,349 participants) in rotator cuff-related shoulder pain found exercise was not clearly better than passive conservative treatments at short-term follow-up, with low-certainty evidence, so patience and a realistic outlook matter.[4]

What this means for you: regular, controlled shoulder exercise is a sensible, well-supported place to start, and Indian club work has early positive evidence for shoulder rotation and strength. The cricket study involved healthy athletes and was small, so treat it as encouraging rather than proof. A few weeks of consistent practice is the fair way to judge whether the routine suits you.

How to get the most out of Ageless Shoulders

  1. Follow the program in order. It is designed for beginners, so start with the first movements even if they look easy.
  2. Start light and slow. The 1 lb club is gentle on purpose; focus on smooth control rather than speed.
  3. Be consistent. Short sessions most days beat one long session. The cricket study ran for six weeks, a sensible window for judging your own results.
  4. Warm up first with a few easy arm circles, and stop if a movement causes sharp pain.
  5. Track how it feels. Notice how easily you reach overhead, behind your back or into a high shelf, and note changes week by week.

Is Ageless Shoulders safe? Side effects

The routine uses a light 1 lb club and gentle movements, which suits most healthy adults. As with any exercise, the most common issue is mild muscle soreness when you first begin.

  • If you have a recent injury, surgery or a diagnosed shoulder problem such as a tear, check with your doctor or physical therapist before starting. The sales page itself suggests following your doctor's orders.
  • Make sure you have space around you so the club can swing freely without hitting people or objects.
  • Stop and seek advice for sharp, worsening or night-time pain, numbness or weakness in the arm, or chest pain or dizziness during exercise.
  • Statements on the sales page about results, safety and the creator are the seller's own and have not been independently verified by us.[5]
