What is the Type 2 Diabetes Strategy and how is it meant to work?

The Type 2 Diabetes Strategy is an e-book written by Jodi Knapp and published through Blue Heron Health News. The author says she began researching diabetes after her mother, who had not yet been diagnosed, collapsed with a very high blood sugar reading. The book is her step-by-step plan, refined over time and updated with newer research.

The plan is built in three steps: reduce foods the author believes drive inflammation, add foods she describes as anti-inflammatory, and make small lifestyle changes. The book also explains the early warning signs of type 2 diabetes, how to read blood sugar numbers, and how to bring favourite foods back using timing.

Food does matter. The ADA-led consensus report says strong evidence supports nutrition therapy as part of quality diabetes care, and that a plan should be individualised.[2] An e-book cannot do the individualising for you, which is why we suggest using it with your doctor or dietitian.

Does a food-and-lifestyle approach work? What the research says

We did not find a published trial of this e-book, and the sales page does not cite studies for its claims. The wider research on food and diabetes is a good deal more encouraging than that:

  • The ADA-led consensus report finds strong evidence that nutrition therapy is effective and cost-effective within diabetes care, and that a personal approach beats a one-size-fits-all plan.[2]
  • In the DiRECT trial of 306 adults with type 2 diabetes, a supervised food-based weight programme led to remission in 46% of people at 12 months versus 4% with usual care, and remission rose with the amount of weight lost.[3]
  • A small study of 11 people found that eight weeks of strict calorie restriction improved beta cell function and reduced fat in the liver and pancreas.[4]

What this means for you: improving how you eat is one of the most powerful things people with type 2 diabetes can do, and an e-book can be a good first step. Those trials were supervised and used set calorie targets, and medication changes in DiRECT were made by clinicians, so treat them as support for the idea rather than proof for this particular book.

How to get the most out of the Type 2 Diabetes Strategy

  1. Tell your doctor first that you plan to change how you eat, especially if you take insulin or other glucose-lowering medicine.
  2. Read the whole book first and then choose two or three changes, rather than changing everything at once.
  3. Keep checking your readings as your care team has asked you to, and share the results with them.
  4. Bring the supplement section to your doctor or pharmacist before you try anything on it.
  5. Judge it within a few weeks, while you are certain to be inside any refund window.

Is the Type 2 Diabetes Strategy safe? What to know first

The Type 2 Diabetes Strategy is a book, so there are no ingredients or doses to check. The main safety point is how it fits with your treatment.

  • Food changes can change how much glucose-lowering medicine you need. If you take insulin or similar medicine, talk to your doctor before you change what you eat.
  • Never stop or reduce prescribed medicine, or skip glucose checks, because of a book. In research, medication changes happened only under clinical supervision.[3]
  • Check any supplement from the book's supplement section with your doctor or pharmacist, as some can interact with medicine.
  • See a doctor urgently for symptoms such as extreme thirst, confusion, fainting or very high or low readings.
  • Dietary supplements are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale.[1]
