What is Nerve Armor and how is it meant to work?

Nerve Armor is a capsule supplement from Critical Nutrition Labs, sold through ClickBank. The seller describes it as a formula to help soothe, protect and support healthy nerves, built around a natural fatty compound called palmitoylethanolamide, or PEA.

PEA is made naturally in the body and is also found in foods such as egg yolk. It has been tested in clinical trials for chronic pain, and that is the strongest research behind this product. Nerve Armor pairs it with alpha lipoic acid, a well-known antioxidant, and benfotiamine, a fat-soluble form of vitamin B1, both of which have been studied in people with diabetic nerve symptoms. Two traditional herbs, gotu kola and corydalis, round out the formula.

The sales page explains the product with a vivid metaphor about overactive immune cells in the nervous system. That is the seller's way of describing it rather than standard medical language, so in this review we focus on what each ingredient has actually been studied for.

Do PEA, alpha lipoic acid and benfotiamine work for nerves?

The research on Nerve Armor's key ingredients is genuinely encouraging:

  • A meta-analysis of randomized trials found that PEA significantly reduced pain in chronic pain overall and in neuropathic pain specifically, with benefits seen from about 4 to 6 weeks.[1]
  • In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of 70 adults with diabetic nerve pain, 600 mg of PEA a day for 8 weeks reduced pain and improved sleep scores compared with placebo, with no safety concerns reported.[2]
  • A pooled analysis of trials found alpha lipoic acid lowered symptom scores in people with diabetic nerve symptoms.[3]
  • Benfotiamine improved the nerve symptom score in a 6-week trial, more clearly at the higher dose and with longer treatment.[4]

What this means for you: PEA is a research-backed ingredient for pain comfort, and Nerve Armor builds on it with two other well-studied nerve nutrients. The studies used particular doses (for example 600 mg of PEA a day) and mostly involved people with diabetes, and the finished formula has not had its own trial, so give it a couple of months of steady use and keep your doctor involved.

How to get the most out of Nerve Armor

  1. Take 2 capsules each morning with a glass of water and a little food, as the seller directs.
  2. Be consistent. The seller suggests 90 days or more, and the studies we reviewed ran for 6 to 8 weeks.
  3. Tell your doctor you are taking it, especially if you manage diabetes or take other medicines.
  4. Support your nerves in other ways: regular gentle walking, balanced meals and good sleep all help.
  5. Keep a simple diary of burning, tingling and sleep so you can see how you are doing over the weeks.

Is Nerve Armor safe? Side effects

In the trials we reviewed, PEA, alpha lipoic acid and benfotiamine were generally well tolerated. Supplements can still affect people differently.

  • If you manage diabetes, take prescription medicine (including medicine for nerve pain), are pregnant or nursing, or are planning surgery, check with your doctor before starting.
  • Dietary supplements are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.
  • Nerve symptoms can have causes that need a proper check. See a doctor for new or worsening numbness, weakness, balance problems, or any loss of bladder or bowel control.

How Nerve Armor compares with other options

  • Prescription medicines: doctors prescribe these for nerve pain and they work for many people, with side effects to weigh. Nerve Armor is a non-prescription option, and you should not change your medicines without your doctor.
  • Single-ingredient supplements: you can buy PEA, alpha lipoic acid or benfotiamine on their own. Nerve Armor's advantage is having them together in one simple serving.
  • Lifestyle and checks: blood sugar control, vitamin level checks, movement and good footwear matter too, and a doctor can tell you which apply to you.
