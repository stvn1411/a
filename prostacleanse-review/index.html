What is ProstaCleanse and how is it meant to work?

ProstaCleanse is a powder you mix with water or a drink you already enjoy, once a day in the morning. A scoop is included in the pack, so the amount is the same every time, and the manufacturer says the formula is stimulant-free and made in an FDA-registered facility that follows good manufacturing practices.

The formula combines a group of plant extracts and nutrients that are commonly used for men's health: stinging nettle root, fenugreek, Tongkat Ali, ashwagandha, cordyceps, maca, Panax ginseng, artichoke and boron, together with vitamin D3, vitamin B6, magnesium and zinc and a small whole-food blend of fruit and vegetable powders.

The website credits the formula to a Dr. Andrew Ellis; we could not independently confirm his credentials, so we have judged the product on its label and the research behind its ingredients instead.

Does ProstaCleanse work?

The research behind the main ingredients is encouraging, especially for nettle root, Tongkat Ali and fenugreek:

  • A 12-month, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of a stinging nettle root extract found a greater improvement in urinary symptom scores than placebo.[2]
  • A separate randomized trial in men gave 450 mg a day of nettle root extract for 12 weeks and also reported improved symptom scores.[3]
  • A placebo-controlled trial of a standardised Tongkat Ali extract in men aged 50 to 70 reported higher total testosterone, and a meta-analysis of randomized trials supports that finding.[4][5]
  • A 12-week placebo-controlled trial of a fenugreek seed extract reported higher testosterone and better sexual function in healthy ageing men.[6]

What this means for you: ProstaCleanse puts several of the best-researched men's health botanicals into one daily drink and shows you the dose of each. Most of the studies used different preparations from the ones in the label, and the finished formula has not had its own clinical trial, so give it a couple of months of daily use alongside your usual routine.

How the 180-day money-back guarantee works

Every order is covered by a 180-day money-back guarantee, a full six months, which makes ProstaCleanse very easy to try. Here is how to use it if you need to:

  1. The 180 days start from the day your order is shipped; your shipping email or packing slip shows the date.
  2. Email support@prostacleanse.org with "Refund Request" in the subject line, and wait for an agent to confirm before you send anything back.
  3. Send all your bottles, empty or unopened, to the returns address you are given, with a note showing your name, order number and email. Return postage is paid by you, and it helps to email the tracking ID.
  4. Once the return is registered you are refunded in full; it usually takes 5 to 10 days to appear on your statement.

Helpful tip: the policy says you can ask for a refund even after finishing your supply, so there is no need to rush. Still, put a reminder in your calendar for around day 170 so you can decide in good time.

Orders are processed by ClickBank, a long-established US online retailer, and ClickBank's order self-service site (clkbank.com) can help with any order question.

How to get the most out of ProstaCleanse

  1. Mix one scoop with water or a favorite drink and take it each morning, as the manufacturer directs.
  2. Be consistent. Many of the studies behind the main ingredients ran for 12 weeks or longer.
  3. Add up your vitamins. The label shows vitamin D3, B6, magnesium and zinc, so check the totals against any multivitamin you take.
  4. Keep a simple diary of night-time trips and how comfortable you feel, so you can see any change over the first few weeks.
  5. Keep up the basics: a balanced diet, regular movement, plenty of water and routine check-ups with your doctor.

Is ProstaCleanse safe? Side effects

ProstaCleanse contains no stimulants according to the manufacturer. Several of its ingredients are active herbs, so a few groups should check first.

  • If you take prescription medicine, or have a thyroid, blood sugar or blood pressure condition, check with your doctor, because herbs such as ashwagandha, ginseng and fenugreek can interact with some medicines.
  • If you are pregnant or nursing, or have been diagnosed with a prostate condition, check with your doctor before starting.
  • Dietary supplements aren't reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[1]
  • See a doctor promptly for blood in urine, pain, fever, difficulty passing urine or a sudden change in symptoms; these need a professional check, not a supplement.
