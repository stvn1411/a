What is The Brain Song and how is it meant to work?

The Brain Song is a roughly 12-minute audio track. The official page describes it as using "advanced sound patterns" to guide the brain toward gamma brainwave frequencies, and links gamma activity to BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor), a protein involved in how brain cells grow and connect.

The closest well-studied technique is binaural beats: two slightly different tones played to each ear, which the brain perceives as a third, pulsing tone. Researchers have tested beats at different frequencies for memory, attention, anxiety and pain, with varied results (more below). The sales page does not say exactly which technique The Brain Song uses, so we treat the binaural-beat research as the best available context rather than direct proof.

The practical appeal is simple: it takes 12 minutes, costs less than a month of most supplements, and fits around a morning coffee or a study session.

Does brainwave audio like The Brain Song work?

Research on this kind of audio is real but mixed, and it is worth knowing both sides:

  • A meta-analysis of 22 studies found an overall medium, significant effect (g = 0.45) of binaural beats on memory, attention, anxiety and pain, and suggested that longer exposure and listening before a task worked better.[2]
  • In a series of three experiments, 16 Hz beta beats showed a modest speed-up in one experiment and a smaller drop in vigilance in another, but the authors' combined analysis did not support a reliable boost to sustained attention.[3]
  • A 2023 trial of 40 Hz gamma beats in 58 healthy adults found no difference in attention or self-rated anxiety compared with a control tone.[4]

What this means for you: audio entrainment is an interesting, actively researched idea, and a short daily listening habit is a pleasant, low-risk way to try it. The Brain Song itself has not had its own clinical trial, so judge it by how you feel after a few weeks of regular use, and use the guarantee if it is not for you.

How to get the most out of The Brain Song

  1. Listen at the same time each day, for example before work or a study session. Research suggests listening before a task can work well.[2]
  2. Use headphones in a quiet spot. Binaural-beat audio generally needs a different tone in each ear.
  3. Keep the volume comfortable. Louder is not better.
  4. Be consistent. Give it several weeks of daily use before you decide.
  5. Support the basics: sleep, movement, hydration and regular breaks do a lot for focus, and the audio works best as an addition to them.

Is The Brain Song safe? Side effects

  • If you have epilepsy or a seizure history, a hearing condition, or use a pacemaker or similar device, check with your doctor before using brainwave audio.

The Brain Song FAQ

Does The Brain Song work?

The Brain Song is built on the idea of audio entrainment, and one meta-analysis of binaural-beat studies found a medium overall effect on memory, attention and anxiety. Later studies, including some on gamma-frequency beats, found little or no attention benefit, and The Brain Song has not had its own clinical trial. Treat it as a low-cost, low-risk routine to try, and results vary from person to person.

Email the vendor with your order number and the reason, and put "Refund Request" in the subject line. The refund page says the window is 60 days, while the sales page says 90 days, so request it within 60 days to be safe. No product has to be returned for a digital order, and refunds can take 5 to 10 business days to show on your statement.

Does The Brain Song have side effects?

Listening to audio at a comfortable volume is generally low risk.
