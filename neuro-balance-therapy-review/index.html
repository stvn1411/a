What is Neuro-Balance Therapy and how is it meant to work?

Neuro-Balance Therapy is not a pill. It is a guided balance program: a video series with a manual, plus an optional DVD set that comes with a small rubber spike ball. Each session starts with a short foot-sole routine while you sit in a chair, then moves into gentle standing and movement sequences at three levels, Beginner, Intermediate and Advanced.

The idea is simple and sensible. Good balance depends on your feet, legs and brain working together, and like any skill it improves with regular practice. By keeping sessions short, the program aims to make that practice easy enough to repeat every day, and it lets you start at a level that suits you and move up when you feel ready.

Because it is a program rather than a supplement, there is nothing to swallow and no ingredient list. What you are buying is the routine, the tool and the guidance.

Does balance training like Neuro-Balance Therapy work?

The research on balance exercise is genuinely encouraging:

  • A Cochrane review of 108 randomized trials with more than 23,000 older adults living at home found that exercise programs lowered the rate of falls by about 23%, with high-certainty evidence.[2]
  • Programs built around balance and functional exercises, the kind of practice this program is built around, were linked to about 24% fewer falls than no exercise.[2]
  • Programs that mixed balance, functional and strength work did even better in the pooled results, so adding gentle leg strengthening alongside the videos is a sensible idea.[2]

What this means for you: regular balance practice has a strong evidence base, and Neuro-Balance Therapy makes it easy to begin and keep going. The program itself has not been tested in its own published clinical trial, and the sales page makes a number of bold statements that we could not verify, so we judge it on the quality of the routine and the low price rather than on those claims.

How to get the most out of Neuro-Balance Therapy

  1. Do it at the same time every day. A morning routine is the easiest habit to keep.
  2. Start at Beginner and move up only when the sequences feel comfortable and steady.
  3. Set up safely: use a sturdy chair, wear non-slip footwear or stand on a non-slip surface, and keep a counter or wall within reach.
  4. Build it up with other movement: walking and light leg-strengthening exercises pair well with balance practice.
  5. Note how you feel after a few weeks, for example how steady you feel when standing from a chair or climbing stairs.

Is Neuro-Balance Therapy safe? Side effects

Gentle balance exercise is suitable for many adults, and the program uses a soft rubber ball and simple movements. The usual care applies to any exercise routine.

  • Check with your doctor or physical therapist first if you have had a recent fall, feel dizzy, or have numbness, pain or weakness in your feet or legs.
  • If you have diabetes, neuropathy or any foot wound or injury, ask your doctor before pressing a spike ball against your foot.
  • The program is not a medical treatment and does not replace professional care for balance disorders.
  • Stop and speak to a professional if you feel pain, sharp discomfort or unusual dizziness.
  • The program is not a dietary supplement, but supplements in general are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale.[1]

How Neuro-Balance Therapy compares with other options

  • Physical therapy: a therapist can assess you personally and tailor exercises, which is the best choice after a fall or for a diagnosed condition. Neuro-Balance Therapy is a more affordable everyday habit that can sit alongside that care.
