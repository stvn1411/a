What is Forward Head Posture FIX and how is it meant to work?

Forward head posture, often called "text neck", is when the head sits in front of the shoulders instead of balanced above them. It is common among people who spend hours at a computer or on a phone, and it can leave the neck and upper back feeling tight and tired.

Forward Head Posture FIX takes a simple, practical approach. Instead of expensive equipment, it uses a 10-exercise "sequential flow" that targets the muscles at the front and back of the neck, including the sternocleidomastoid, with muscle re-education drills to help your head sit better over your spine. The video lessons are split into a coaching version that explains each move, a follow-along version, and swaps that make exercises easier or harder.

It is a program, not a pill, so the results come from you doing the moves consistently, which is exactly why a routine of under 15 minutes is so appealing.

Does neck exercise for forward head posture work?

The idea behind the program, that targeted neck exercise can help posture, is well supported by published research:

  • A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 10 randomized trials with 550 people who had forward head posture and neck pain. Therapeutic exercise improved head alignment, reduced pain and improved neck function compared with control groups.[3]
  • In a randomized trial of 58 adults with chronic neck pain, a 6-week program training the deep neck flexor muscles helped people keep a more upright neck posture during computer work.[2]

What this means for you: exercise is a research-backed way to work on forward head posture, and this program packages it into a short, guided routine. The program itself hasn't been tested in its own trial, and the sales page makes broader health claims than we can support, so we recommend it as a posture and neck-comfort routine and suggest giving it a few weeks of regular use.

How to get the most out of Forward Head Posture FIX

  1. Start with the coaching video so you learn the right form before using the follow-along version.
  2. Be consistent. A short daily session beats an occasional long one; studies measured results after several weeks.
  3. Use the easier swaps if a move feels too strong at first, and progress gradually.
  4. Set up your workspace: screen at eye level, feet flat, and short breaks every 30 to 60 minutes.
  5. Check progress with a side-on photo each week, with your ear over your shoulder as the goal.

Is Forward Head Posture FIX safe? Side effects

Gentle neck exercise is generally safe for healthy adults, but it should never be painful. Move slowly and stop if something feels wrong.

  • If you have a neck injury, disc problem, osteoporosis or have had neck surgery, check with your doctor or physical therapist before starting.
  • See a doctor promptly for numbness, tingling or weakness in your arms or hands, new severe headaches, dizziness or pain after an injury.
  • This is an exercise program, not a treatment for any condition, and it does not replace a diagnosis from a health professional.
