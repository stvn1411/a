What is The Prostate Protocol and how is it meant to work?

The Prostate Protocol is a guide, not a supplement. There are no pills to take: you read the plan and follow it. It is written by Scott Davis and published by Blue Heron Health News, and it is built around the idea that what a man eats day to day influences how his prostate and urinary system feel as he ages.

The author's explanation is that certain foods help and others hurt the way the prostate handles hormones, and the plan lays out, day by day, what to eat and what to avoid. That specific mechanism is the author's own model rather than an established medical one, but the broader idea behind it is well supported: reviews of the research find that healthier eating, a healthy weight and regular physical activity are linked with lower risk of urinary symptoms in men.[1]

Because it is a plain-English plan using ordinary groceries, it is easy to start the same day you order.

Does the diet-and-lifestyle approach behind it work?

We could not find a published trial of The Prostate Protocol itself, so we looked at the evidence on the approach it takes: diet and lifestyle for urinary symptoms in men.

  • A review in Current Opinion in Urology found that modifiable lifestyle factors, including obesity, physical activity and diet, significantly influence the risk of symptomatic benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) and urinary symptoms.[1]
  • The same review found that more physical activity and vegetable consumption were linked with lower risk, while obesity and higher meat and fat consumption were linked with higher risk.[1]
  • A second review reached a similar conclusion: healthy eating and regular activity are repeatedly linked with a lower risk of symptoms or of symptoms getting worse, and promoting a healthy lifestyle is beneficial for cardiovascular and overall health too.[2]
  • Both reviews note that randomized trials of lifestyle changes for urinary symptoms were still needed when they were written.[1][2]

What this means for you: the general direction of the research supports what this plan asks you to do: eat well, stay active and keep a healthy weight. The specific claims on the sales page come from the author and from individual testimonials, so treat the plan as a structured way to build healthy habits, and keep seeing your doctor for any prostate or urinary concern.[2]

Is The Prostate Protocol safe?

The plan is food and lifestyle based, with no pills or devices, so it is gentle compared with many options. It is still wise to involve your doctor.

  • Check with your doctor first if you take prostate medication, blood thinners or other prescriptions, or have a medical condition that needs a special diet.
  • Prostate symptoms can have several causes. Blood in the urine, pain, fever, being unable to pass urine, or a raised PSA result should always be checked by a doctor.
  • Do not stop or change prescribed treatment because of any program without talking to your doctor.
  • The program is not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, and nothing here replaces medical care.
