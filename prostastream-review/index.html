What is Prostastream and how is it meant to work?

Prostastream is a prostate supplement created around the belief that plants can help men look after their prostate. The sales page is written by its creator, who describes a formula of familiar botanicals and several medicinal mushrooms.

The prostate is a small gland below the bladder that tends to grow with age, which is why many men over 40 notice more night-time bathroom trips. Prostastream is designed to support everyday prostate health, not to diagnose or treat any condition.

It is a dietary supplement sold only through its official page, with a one-time payment, no subscription and free US shipping.

Does Prostastream work? What the research says

Three of the named ingredients in Prostastream have solid research behind them for urinary comfort in men:

  • A systematic review of 18 randomized trials in 2,939 men found saw palmetto reduced night-time urination and improved peak flow compared with placebo, with mild and infrequent side effects.[2]
  • A meta-analysis of pygeum found nocturia reduced by about 19% and peak urine flow up by about 23%, with side effects similar to placebo.[4]
  • A 6-month trial of stinging nettle in 620 men reported improved symptom scores and flow compared with placebo, although the author asks for confirming studies.[5]
  • The mushrooms and other botanicals are popular for general wellness; they are not the main reason for our score.

What this means for you: the core botanicals in Prostastream have genuine research behind them for urinary comfort in men. The finished formula has not had its own clinical trial and the references on the official page are general background rather than studies of the product, so give it a few weeks of steady use and judge for yourself within the 60-day guarantee.

Is Prostastream safe? Side effects

Botanical prostate supplements are generally well tolerated. In the saw palmetto research, the most frequent side effects were mild stomach upsets.[3]

  • If you are pregnant, nursing or take medication or have a medical condition, check with your doctor before starting, as the official page itself advises.
  • See a doctor for pain, blood in the urine, a very weak stream or being unable to pass urine.
  • A supplement is not a substitute for prostate screening or advice from your doctor.
  • Dietary supplements are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[1]
