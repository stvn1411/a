What is Cleanest Body and how is it meant to work?

Cleanest Body is a herbal liquid that you take with the dropper supplied. You fill it once a day and add it to water, tea or orange juice. Because it is a liquid, there is nothing to swallow, and it takes about five seconds to prepare.

The formula blends 11 herbs and plant oils that have a long history in traditional digestive and wellness routines. Fennel, peppermint and slippery elm are common choices for digestive comfort, pumpkin seed and papaya seed are used in traditional gut-health habits, and garlic, oregano and clove are kitchen staples with a long culinary record.

The manufacturer describes it as a cleansing formula that supports a healthy weight and gut. We review it as a daily herbal routine: a supportive product to go with balanced meals, activity and good sleep, not a medicine or a replacement for medical care.

Do the herbs in Cleanest Body have research behind them?

Research on herbal blends is more limited than for single, standardized ingredients, but two of the herbs in this formula stand out:

  • Peppermint oil: a meta-analysis of five randomized controlled trials with 392 people found it significantly better than placebo for overall improvement of irritable bowel symptoms, with mild and short-lived side effects.[2]
  • Fennel: a small trial reported that fennel tea reduced short-term subjective appetite in overweight women.[4]
  • The remaining herbs are best known from traditional and culinary use, with a good deal of laboratory research and fewer human trials.
  • None of these studies tested Cleanest Body itself, and the doses used are not the same as the doses in the formula, which are not disclosed.

What this means for you: Cleanest Body is built from familiar herbs, two of which have encouraging human research for digestive comfort and appetite. Because the finished formula has not been tested on its own, treat it as supportive and give it a few months alongside balanced meals and regular activity. The manufacturer itself suggests at least 3 months before judging.

Is Cleanest Body safe? Side effects

Cleanest Body is made from herbs and plant oils that are also used in cooking, and the manufacturer says it has no stimulants or added chemicals. Some herbs in the blend are potent, and mild digestive changes are the most common complaint with herbal formulas.

  • If you are pregnant, nursing or planning a pregnancy, check with your doctor first. Some of the herbs, such as wormwood, are not recommended in pregnancy.
  • If you take prescription medicine, including blood thinners or diabetes medicine, ask your doctor first, since garlic and other herbs can interact with some medicines.
  • If you are allergic to any of the 11 herbs, or to related plants such as ragweed, do not use it.
  • Dietary supplements are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[1]
  • See a doctor for persistent stomach pain, blood in the stool, unexplained weight loss or ongoing digestive symptoms.
