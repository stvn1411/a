What is Roll On Relief and how is it meant to work?

Roll On Relief is a liquid pain reliever in a bottle with a rolling-ball tip, a bit like a deodorant roll-on. You roll it over the sore area and the formula goes on exactly where you aim it, with no need to touch it or wash your hands afterwards. The bottle is about four inches long, so it is also easy to reach the middle of your own back.

Its main ingredients, camphor and menthol, are counterirritants. They create a cooling-then-warming feeling on the skin that takes your attention away from the ache underneath. This is the same basic approach behind many familiar drugstore rubs, and both ingredients appear in the U.S. FDA's over-the-counter monograph for external analgesics.[1]

The rest of the formula is a soothing base of aloe vera gel and MCT oil, with small amounts of tea tree oil, peppermint oil and a few botanicals.

Does a camphor and menthol roll-on work?

Counterirritant pain relievers are among the most widely used topical options, and the research on menthol is encouraging:

  • In a triple-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of 10 workers with chronic arm and hand pain, menthol gel reduced pain intensity more than a placebo gel, with about 1.2 points more relief on a 0–10 scale.[2]
  • In a randomized, double-blind trial of 208 adults with mild to moderate muscle strain, a patch with 10% methyl salicylate and 3% menthol gave about 40% more pain relief over 8 hours than a placebo patch, with similar rates of side effects.[3]
  • Camphor and menthol are both recognized as counterirritant ingredients in the FDA's external analgesic monograph.[1]

What this means for you: a menthol-and-camphor roll-on is a sensible, research-supported way to take the edge off everyday aches. These trials tested other formulas (a gel and a patch), and Roll On Relief itself has not had its own clinical trial, so treat it as a comfortable, low-risk option and see how it works for you.

How to get the most out of Roll On Relief

  1. Roll it onto clean, dry, unbroken skin over the sore area. Adults and children over 12 can use it up to 4 times a day.
  2. Wash your hands if they touch the area, and keep it away from eyes and the inside of your nose and mouth.
  3. Don't combine it with heat. The label says not to use it with a heating pad or other heating device, and the formula is flammable, so keep it away from open flame.
  4. Use it on its own. The label advises against layering it with other ointments, creams or sprays on the same spot.
  5. Pair it with gentle movement and rest as your doctor or physio suggests, and note how the area feels over the first week.

Is Roll On Relief safe? Side effects

Used as directed, topical counterirritants are generally well tolerated. The most common issue is mild skin warmth, redness or irritation where it's applied.

  • For external use only. Avoid eyes and mucous membranes, and don't apply it to wounds or damaged skin.
  • If you are pregnant or breast-feeding, ask a health professional before using it, and check with a doctor before giving it to a child aged 12 or under.
  • Stop using it and speak to a doctor if the condition gets worse or lasts more than 7 days.
  • Keep it out of reach of children and don't use it if the safety seal is broken.
  • Statements about this product have not been evaluated by the FDA.[5]

How Roll On Relief compares with other options

  • Creams and gels: classic rubs do the same counterirritant job but you apply them with your fingers. Roll On Relief's applicator is cleaner and easier on your own back.
  • Topical NSAID gels: these work differently and are an option to discuss with your pharmacist or doctor; Roll On Relief contains no NSAIDs.
  • Pills: oral pain relievers act throughout your body, while a roll-on stays on the sore spot and nothing is swallowed.
