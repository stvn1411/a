What is All Day Slimming Tea and how is it meant to work?

All Day Slimming Tea is a two-part routine. A morning tea, built around green and oolong tea, is meant to be sipped during the day. An evening tea, made with peppermint, fennel and ginger, is meant for after dinner.

The idea is straightforward: herbal teas are a pleasant way to build a daily habit, replace sugary drinks and support digestion, and some of the ingredients, such as green tea catechins, have been studied for their effect on body weight.

The official page lists the full recipe for both blends. It is sold through ClickBank as a one-time payment.

Does All Day Slimming Tea work? What the research says

No clinical trial of the finished tea has been published, so we looked at its best-studied ingredient:

  • A meta-analysis of 15 randomized trials found that green tea catechins taken with caffeine gave <strong>modest reductions in body weight, BMI and waist size</strong>, compared with caffeine alone.[2]
  • The same review found no clear benefit from catechins without caffeine, so the natural caffeine in green and oolong tea is part of the picture.[2]
  • Effects in these studies were modest, which fits a realistic view of tea as a helpful habit alongside healthy eating and movement, not a shortcut.

What this means for you: The tea is built around a well-studied ingredient and makes a pleasant routine. Think of it as a companion to healthy eating and regular movement, with results varying from person to person. The official page recommends drinking it daily for 3 to 6 months.

  2. <strong>Start gently</strong> and see how your digestion responds, especially to the evening tea.

Is All Day Slimming Tea safe? Side effects

Herbal teas are generally well tolerated by healthy adults. The morning tea contains natural caffeine from green and oolong tea, and the evening tea includes senna, which is a laxative herb.

  • If you are <strong>pregnant, nursing or under 18</strong>, or have a digestive, blood sugar or blood pressure condition, check with your doctor before starting.
  • If you take <strong>prescription medicine</strong>, ask your doctor first, since licorice root, senna and green tea can interact with some medicines.
  • Loose stools or stomach cramps can happen with senna. Reduce or stop and ask a doctor if they continue.
  • Dietary supplements aren't reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[1]
