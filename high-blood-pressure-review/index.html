What is the Blood Pressure Program and how is it meant to work?

The Blood Pressure Program is a set of three guided audio exercises that you follow by pressing play. There's nothing to learn or master: the narrator talks you through each one, and a PDF explains the steps if you'd rather read.

The first exercise, focused walking or tapping, takes about 9 minutes and is the one the author calls required. The second, emotional release, uses guided breathing and runs about 22 minutes. The third is a 24-minute deep relaxation you can use as a wind-down at night. The author says to use the second and third as often or as little as you like.

The approach sits within a well-known field of research: slow breathing, meditation and gentle mind-body movement have all been studied for their effect on blood pressure. The program is meant to be used alongside medical care, not instead of it.

Does the Blood Pressure Program work? What the research on mind-body practice shows

We could not find a published trial on this specific program, so we looked at research on the kinds of practice it uses:

  • A meta-analysis of 17 randomized trials of device-guided and non-device slow breathing found average reductions of about 5.6 mm Hg systolic and 3.0 mm Hg diastolic, which the authors called modest.[2]
  • A meta-analysis of nine Transcendental Meditation trials estimated falls of about 4.7 and 3.2 mm Hg in systolic and diastolic pressure.[4]
  • A network meta-analysis of 49 trials found tai chi and other traditional mind-body exercises were among the best-performing options for systolic pressure in middle-aged and older adults.[5]
  • Not every result is as strong: an analysis limited to blinded, sham-controlled breathing-device trials found no significant drop in office readings, so effects depend on the person and the practice.[3]

What this means for you: relaxation and slow-breathing practices are a well-studied, low-risk way to support healthy blood pressure, and this program makes them easy to follow every day. Think of it as a companion to your doctor's plan, not a replacement. Keep taking any prescribed medicine and ask your doctor before changing it.

How to get the most out of the Blood Pressure Program

  1. Start with Exercise 1. The author calls it the most important one, and it only takes about 9 minutes.
  2. Pick a regular time, such as after waking or in a quiet afternoon break, so it becomes a habit.
  3. Use Exercises 2 and 3 as you like. Many people save the 24-minute relaxation for bedtime.
  4. Check your blood pressure at home if your doctor recommends it, and share the readings at your next appointment.
  5. Keep up the basics: take prescribed medicine as directed, eat well, stay active and keep your appointments.

Is the Blood Pressure Program safe? Side effects

Guided breathing and relaxation are generally safe for most healthy adults. A few points are worth keeping in mind:

  • Never stop or change blood pressure medicine because of a program. Talk to your doctor first.
  • Very high readings, chest pain, severe headache, vision changes or shortness of breath need urgent medical care, not an audio program.
  • If you feel dizzy while breathing slowly, stop and sit down. Pregnant, or have a heart condition? Check with your doctor first.
  • The program is not a dietary supplement, but the FDA's general advice on health claims is worth knowing.[1]
