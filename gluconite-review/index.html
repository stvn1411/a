What is Gluconite and how is it meant to work?

Gluconite is a powder you stir into a glass of water about 30 to 60 minutes before bed. The maker describes it as a sleep and metabolism formula, and positions it as support for healthy blood sugar while you sleep. It is sold as a dietary supplement, not a medicine.

The idea behind it is simple and sensible: sleep and metabolism are linked. A 2022 meta-analysis of randomized trials found that cutting sleep short reduced insulin sensitivity in healthy adults, which is one reason good sleep habits are a standard part of a healthy routine.[2] Gluconite pairs calming ingredients with a few everyday nutrients so that one evening drink covers both goals.

Unlike many supplements, the supplement facts panel on the official site lists a dose for every single ingredient. That makes it easy to compare it with other bedtime products and to check any ingredient with your doctor or pharmacist.

Does Gluconite work? What the research says

We did not find a published clinical trial of the finished Gluconite formula, so the best we can do is look at the research on its main ingredients and on sleep. That research is encouraging in places:

  • A meta-analysis of 35 randomized trials on sleep manipulation found that restricting sleep reduced insulin sensitivity, which supports the idea of building a consistent sleep routine.[2]
  • A meta-analysis of 8 randomized trials in adults with type 2 diabetes found that melatonin was linked with lower fasting glucose (about 12.7 mg/dL on average) and lower fasting insulin. The authors note that earlier research was inconclusive and that trials differed.[3]
  • A small single-blind trial of 64 adults with type 2 diabetes found lower HbA1c and insulin after 8 weeks of chamomile tea three times a day.[5]
  • Not every trial agrees: in a triple-blind trial of 70 adults, 6 mg of melatonin nightly for 8 weeks did not change glucose or HbA1c compared with placebo.[4]

What this means for you: the strongest, most consistent benefit to expect is a calmer evening routine and better sleep habits, with possible extra support for healthy metabolism. Gluconite uses lower amounts than some of the studies (one scoop has 500 mg of chamomile powder, while the tea trial used about 9 g a day), so treat the research as encouraging rather than a promise.

How the 180-day money-back guarantee works

Gluconite is covered by a 180-day guarantee, one of the longest we have seen on a supplement. The refund policy page sets out the steps:

  1. The 180 days start from the day your order ships. Your shipping email or order slip shows the date.
  2. Email the support team with "Refund Request" in the subject line, and wait for an agent to confirm before you send anything back.
  3. Send all your bottles, empty or unopened, to the returns address you are given. No return label is provided and you pay the postage. Email the tracking number with your name, order number and order email.
  4. Once the return is registered you are refunded in full, and the refund usually shows on your statement within 5 to 10 days.

Helpful tip: the window is generous, so you can use the full three months the maker suggests and still have time left. Put a reminder in your calendar for around day 150 so you can decide comfortably.

Orders are processed by ClickBank, a long-established US online retailer, and ClickBank's order support can also help with any order question.

How to get the most out of Gluconite

  1. Take it at the same time each night, 30 to 60 minutes before bed, in 6 oz of water.
  2. Keep it a wind-down ritual: dim the lights and put screens away so the drink supports a calm evening.
  3. Give it time. The maker suggests at least three months, and your 180-day guarantee covers that comfortably.
  4. Keep the basics going: balanced meals, regular activity and a steady sleep schedule matter more than any supplement.
  5. Keep notes: how quickly you fall asleep, how you feel in the morning, and any readings your own care team asks you to track.

Is Gluconite safe? Side effects

The maker lists no reported side effects and says the formula is made in an FDA-registered facility in the USA. Because it contains melatonin and other relaxing ingredients, drowsiness is the effect to plan for.

  • Do not take it before driving or operating machinery.
  • The maker advises against it if you are pregnant, nursing or under 18.
  • If you take medicine for blood sugar, blood pressure, sleep or anxiety, or use blood thinners, check with your doctor or pharmacist first. White willow bark is related to aspirin-type compounds, so tell them if you are sensitive to aspirin.
  • Do not change or stop prescribed diabetes medicine because you started a supplement.
  • Dietary supplements aren't reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[1]
