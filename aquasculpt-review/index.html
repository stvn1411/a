Does AquaSculpt work? What the research says

The research on the ingredient types the official page cites is encouraging, and the effects are modest and consistent:

  • A meta-analysis of 3 randomized trials found that green coffee bean extract providing at least 500 mg a day of chlorogenic acid lowered body weight by about 1.3 kg.[2]
  • Across 15 randomized trials, green tea catechins taken with caffeine reduced body weight, BMI and waist circumference compared with caffeine alone.[3]
  • A meta-analysis of 9 randomized trials found that L-carnitine users lost more weight than controls.[4]
  • The authors of these reviews describe the effects as modest and call for longer trials, which is why we see these ingredients as support for a healthy lifestyle, not a replacement for one.[3]

What this means for you: the ingredient types behind AquaSculpt have genuine human research showing small but real effects on weight. We could not verify the exact formula or doses on the pages we opened, so read the label at checkout and use it alongside balanced meals and regular movement.

  2. Be consistent. The studies we cite typically ran for several weeks to months.
  3. Pair it with the basics: balanced meals, enough protein and fibre, regular exercise and good sleep.

Is AquaSculpt safe? Side effects

Supplements built around green coffee, green tea extract and similar ingredients are generally well tolerated by healthy adults, but a few precautions are worth knowing.

  • Caffeine-containing ingredients can cause jitteriness, trouble sleeping or a faster heartbeat in sensitive people; check with your doctor first if you are sensitive to caffeine or have a heart condition.
  • Some of these ingredients can interact with medicines, so check with your doctor first if you take prescription medicine.
  • If you are pregnant, nursing or have a medical condition, ask your doctor before starting.
  • Dietary supplements are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[1]
