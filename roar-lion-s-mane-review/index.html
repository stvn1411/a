What is Roar Lion's Mane and how is it meant to work?

Roar Lion's Mane is a capsule supplement built around Hericium erinaceus, the shaggy white mushroom known as lion's mane. The brand describes its lion's mane as a high-potency fruiting body extract made with dual extraction, and pairs it with reishi, chaga and other functional mushrooms.

Lion's mane has attracted attention because laboratory work suggests it may influence nerve-supporting compounds, and a few small human trials have looked at focus, processing speed and mood. A recent systematic review describes the clinical evidence as preliminary: promising, but still few and small studies.[4]

We think the sensible way to see Roar Lion's Mane is as a daily wellness supplement. The brand's own page makes broad claims about brain cleaning and long-term brain health that go well beyond what the published trials show, so we focus here on what the research does support.

Does lion's mane work?

The human research is still small, but there are encouraging signals:

  • In a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 30 Japanese adults aged 50 to 80 with mild cognitive impairment, those taking lion's mane tablets for 16 weeks scored significantly higher on a cognitive scale than the placebo group at weeks 8, 12 and 16. Laboratory tests showed no adverse effect.[1]
  • In a pilot study of 41 to 43 healthy adults aged 18 to 45 taking 1.8 g a day, a single dose was followed by faster Stroop-task performance at 60 minutes, and the 28-day group showed a trend toward lower subjective stress. The authors call the results tentative.[2]
  • A 2025 crossover study of 18 healthy adults did not find an overall improvement after a single 3 g dose, although one task (a pegboard test) improved at 90 minutes. The authors suggest any benefit may depend on the task and call for longer studies.[3]

What this means for you: lion's mane is a reasonable, well-studied choice for people who want to support everyday focus, and Roar Lion's Mane puts it in an easy once-a-day format. The research is early, the trials used other doses, and the finished formula has not had its own clinical trial yet, so think of it as a supportive habit and judge it over a month or two.

How to get the most out of Roar Lion's Mane

  1. Follow the directions: two capsules once a day with 8 oz of water, in the afternoon or about 2 hours before bed. The page also says you may mix them into a smoothie.
  2. Be consistent. The lion's mane trials we cite ran from 4 to 16 weeks, so give it at least a month or two.
  3. Keep the basics in place: sleep, regular movement, a varied diet and staying socially and mentally active all matter for how you feel and think.
  4. Keep a simple note of your focus, mood and energy once a week so you can judge it fairly before the guarantee ends.
  5. Tell your doctor you are taking it if you use prescription medicine.

Is Roar Lion's Mane safe? Side effects

Mushroom supplements are generally well tolerated by healthy adults. A 2025 systematic review notes that side effects of lion's mane are often not reported, and lists possible stomach discomfort, headache and allergic reactions.[4]

  • If you are pregnant or nursing, allergic to mushrooms, or take prescription medicine, check with your doctor before starting.
  • Dietary supplements aren't reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[5]
  • See a doctor for memory problems, confusion or other symptoms that worry you. A supplement is not a substitute for a medical check.
