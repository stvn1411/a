What is Anabolic Running and how is it meant to work?

Anabolic Running is a digital training program written by Joe LoGalbo. Instead of long jogs, it asks you to run in short bursts at a very high effort, with rest in between, for about 16 minutes of running a week. It also teaches a nasal-breathing technique to use during the hard efforts and adds a few short bodyweight exercises.

The idea behind it has real support in exercise science. Researchers have studied short, hard intervals for years, and they find that they can improve fitness and body composition in a fraction of the time that steady cardio takes (more on that below). Anabolic Running packages that approach into a ready-made plan for men, so you don't have to design the workouts yourself.

The sales page also makes claims about testosterone and growth hormone. We looked at the exercise research rather than those specific claims, and we judge the program on what it actually asks you to do.

Does short interval training work?

The research on short interval training is encouraging, especially for saving time:

  • A meta-analysis of 21 studies with 849 participants found that low-volume interval training improved cardiorespiratory fitness, with results comparable to longer moderate-intensity training, while taking only a fraction of the time.[2]
  • A review of high-intensity interval training versus moderate continuous training in overweight and obese adults found similar reductions in fat mass and waist size, with intervals needing about 40% less training time.[3]
  • A later meta-analysis of 11 randomized trials found that intervals were not better than continuous training for body-fat percentage, which means the main advantage is saving time rather than extra fat loss.[4]

What this means for you: if you want to keep your fitness up with less time on the treadmill, short interval training is a well-supported way to do it, and Anabolic Running gives you a ready-made plan. Think of it as an efficient workout rather than a shortcut to rapid results, and combine it with sensible eating and sleep.

How to get the most out of Anabolic Running

  1. Warm up properly before every hard effort, so your muscles and joints are ready.
  2. Start at a level that suits you. If you are new to hard running, build up gradually.
  3. Be consistent. Interval studies usually measure results after several weeks of regular sessions.
  4. Add some strength work. The bonus bodyweight guide is a simple way to do this.
  5. Support it with sleep and food. Recovery is when your body adapts.

Is Anabolic Running safe?

Short interval workouts are safe for many healthy adults when built up gradually, but they are demanding. The main risks are muscle and joint strain, and overexertion if you push too hard too soon.

  • If you have heart disease, high blood pressure, chest pain, joint problems or any other medical condition, check with your doctor before starting.
  • If you have not exercised for a long time, build up gradually and consider a health check first.
  • Stop and seek medical help for chest pain, dizziness, fainting or unusual shortness of breath.
  • If you are worried about low testosterone or sex drive, see your doctor; an exercise program is not a medical test or treatment.[1]

How Anabolic Running compares with other options

  • Long steady cardio: great for health and endurance, but it takes much more time. Research suggests intervals give similar fitness gains in less time.[2]
  • Free interval workouts: you can find interval plans online. Anabolic Running's advantage is a complete, structured plan with bonuses for a very small price.
  • Gym and strength training: weights build muscle in a way running does not, so combine the two if you can.
