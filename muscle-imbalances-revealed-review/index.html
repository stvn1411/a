What is Muscle Imbalances Revealed and how is it meant to work?

Muscle Imbalances Revealed, often shortened to MIR, is a video course about a simple idea: when one muscle group is tight or weak, nearby joints can end up working harder than they should. The Lower Body System, now in its third edition, focuses on the hips and pelvis, knees, ankles and shoulders, and teaches how to look for these patterns and what exercises and mobility work to use.

It was created as a continuing-education resource for fitness professionals, so the tone is practical and a little technical. The team behind it includes Rick Kaselj, an exercise and injury specialist, and coaches Mike Robertson, Bill Hartman, Eric Beard, Kevin Yates and Dean Somerset. The sales page lists the program as a set of video presentations plus handouts, with sessions on hip and pelvis imbalances, mobility, knee strength and stability, identifying and addressing lower-body imbalances, the shoulder, fascia, corrective exercise for performance and hip and ankle mobility.

Everything is a digital download: you pay once, save the files and keep them. For anyone who likes to understand why they are doing an exercise, not just copy a routine, that format works well.

Does the approach behind Muscle Imbalances Revealed work?

The course is built on ideas that are well supported in the exercise research:

  • A meta-analysis of 11 randomized trials and one non-randomized study (604 people) found that strengthening the hip muscles reduced pain in people with patellofemoral (front-of-knee) pain, and that combining hip and knee strengthening improved function more than knee exercises alone.[1]
  • A systematic review of 42 randomized trials found that regular static stretching gave small but significant gains in maximal strength and muscle size, with longer stretching durations and more frequent sessions working best.[2]
  • The authors of the hip review point out that most studies were of low methodological quality, so the results should be seen as encouraging rather than final.[1]

What this means for you: the building blocks of this course, hip strengthening and regular mobility work, have real research behind them. The course itself has not had a clinical trial, so treat it as a well-organised education tool and give it a few weeks of consistent practice.

How to get the most out of Muscle Imbalances Revealed

  1. Start with the assessment sessions. Understanding what to look for makes the exercise sessions easier to use.
  2. Take notes on your own movement, such as which hip feels tight, and revisit them after a few weeks.
  3. Be consistent. Mobility and strengthening work builds over weeks, not days.
  4. Start light and build gradually. Use the handouts to keep technique tidy before adding load.
  5. Pair it with your usual training rather than replacing it, and keep a simple log of what you do.

Is Muscle Imbalances Revealed safe? Things to know

Exercise and mobility work is generally safe for healthy adults when started gradually. As with any exercise program, listen to your body.

  • If you have pain, a recent injury, a joint condition or have had surgery, check with your doctor or physiotherapist before starting.
  • Stop any movement that causes sharp or worsening pain.
  • See a professional for swelling, numbness, pain that wakes you at night, or pain that does not settle.
  • Health and fitness products are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness, and the course makes no medical claims we could verify beyond the research cited here.
