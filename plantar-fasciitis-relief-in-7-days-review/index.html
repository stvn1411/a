What is Plantar Fasciitis Relief in 7 Days and how is it meant to work?

Plantar fasciitis is irritation of the thick band of tissue that runs along the sole of the foot, and it is one of the most common causes of heel pain. It is typically felt as a sharp pain with the first steps of the day or after sitting for a while.

Plantar Fasciitis Relief in 7 Days is an instant-access digital program of video lessons and manuals. Home programs for this condition typically centre on stretching and strengthening the foot and calf, and the best-known clinical research on plantar fasciitis tested stretching, which we cover in the research section below. Do stretching programs for plantar fasciitis work?

The research behind this type of routine is some of the more encouraging evidence among home treatments for heel pain:

  • In a randomized trial of 101 people with chronic heel pain, a plantar fascia-specific stretching program gave greater improvements in pain, activity limitation and satisfaction than Achilles tendon stretching alone.[1]
  • A two-year follow-up of people who used the plantar fascia stretching program concluded it should be a key part of treatment for chronic plantar fasciitis.[2]
  • In a trial of people with acute symptoms, plantar fascia-specific stretching produced greater improvement at two months than radial shock-wave therapy.[3]

What this means for you: stretching programs aimed at the plantar fascia have good trial support, and this program puts a short routine of that kind into video and manuals at a very low price. The study programs ran for weeks to months, so treat the seven days in the title as a starting point and keep going for about two months.

How to get the most out of Plantar Fasciitis Relief in 7 Days

  1. Stretch before your first steps in the morning, when plantar fasciitis is usually worst.
  2. Be consistent. The trials followed stretching programs for weeks, so keep going past day seven.
  3. Wear supportive shoes and avoid long periods of barefoot walking on hard floors while you recover.
  4. Ease off if it hurts sharply. Stretching should feel like tension, not stabbing pain.
  5. Keep a simple log of morning pain so you can judge progress honestly before the refund window closes.
  6. Return to exercise gradually as heel pain settles.

Is Plantar Fasciitis Relief in 7 Days safe? What to know first

Gentle stretching is generally low risk. As with any exercise routine, start slowly and listen to your body.

  • See a doctor or podiatrist for severe pain, swelling, bruising, numbness or pain after an injury, as these can point to a different problem.
  • If you have diabetes, circulation problems or a nerve condition in the feet, ask your doctor before starting.
  • If heel pain has lasted many months despite home care, get it assessed, since other options such as footwear changes, orthotics or physical therapy may be worth discussing.
  • The title and sales copy make strong claims. Treat them as marketing and keep your healthcare team informed.
