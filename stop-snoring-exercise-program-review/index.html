What is Stop Snoring Exercise Program and how is it meant to work?

The Stop Snoring Exercise Program is a digital guide that teaches a series of simple exercises for the muscles of the tongue, soft palate, jaw and throat. The idea is straightforward: snoring happens when tissues in the upper airway relax and vibrate as you breathe during sleep, so toning those muscles may help keep the airway steadier.

The author, Christian Goodman, says he built the program after dealing with chronic snoring himself, and that the exercises were inspired by his wife's work as a voice teacher. The official page says the program has been running for about ten years.

Because it is a download, you can start the same day, and the exercises need no gadgets. That makes it very easy to fit into a morning routine, a commute or time in front of the TV, which is how the author suggests using it.

Does Stop Snoring Exercise Program work?

Research into throat and tongue exercises (often called oropharyngeal or myofunctional therapy) is encouraging, although the studies are small:

  • In a randomized trial of 31 adults with moderate obstructive sleep apnea, three months of daily oropharyngeal exercises reduced the severity of the condition and its symptoms compared with sham therapy.[1]
  • A systematic review and meta-analysis of myofunctional therapy reported that it lowered the apnea-hypopnea index by roughly half in adults and suggested it could serve as an add-on to other treatments.[2]
  • A later Cochrane review of nine studies found that, compared with sham therapy, these exercises probably reduce daytime sleepiness and may improve sleep quality in the short term, while rating much of the evidence as low certainty.[3]

What this means for you: exercise-based approaches have real research behind them, and a short daily routine costs very little to try. The studies tested similar exercises in people with sleep apnea rather than this guide, so treat it as a sensible add-on to medical care, give it several weeks of daily practice, and keep your clinician in the loop.

How to get the most out of Stop Snoring Exercise Program

  1. Start with the short routine. Pick a time you already have, such as after brushing or at a red light, and attach the exercises to it.
  2. Practise every day. The studies we found used daily exercises over weeks to months.
  3. Track what changes. Ask a partner or use a free snore-recording app to compare week one with week six.
  4. Add healthy sleep habits: a regular bedtime, sleeping on your side and avoiding alcohol close to bed can all help.
  5. Tell your doctor if you are using CPAP or another treatment, and keep using it unless they advise otherwise.

Is Stop Snoring Exercise Program safe? Side effects

The program is made of gentle exercises, so serious side effects are not expected for most people. You may feel mild tiredness in the tongue, jaw or throat muscles when you start, much like any new exercise.

The official page is clear that the program is not a medical device, medication or medical advice.

  • If you have been diagnosed with sleep apnea, keep using any prescribed treatment such as CPAP and talk to your clinician before changing anything. The official page says the same.
  • Loud snoring with pauses in breathing, gasping, heavy daytime sleepiness or high blood pressure deserves a proper sleep assessment from a doctor.
  • Stop and check with a professional if exercises cause pain in the jaw, tongue or throat.

How Stop Snoring Exercise Program compares with other options

  • CPAP and oral appliances: these are the usual medical options for diagnosed sleep apnea and are fitted by a clinician. The program is a low-cost add-on rather than a replacement.
  • Nasal strips and sprays: can help some people with nasal blockage; the program works on a different part of the problem, the throat muscles.
  • Supervised myofunctional therapy: a therapist can tailor exercises and check your technique, which costs more; this guide is the budget, self-paced option.
