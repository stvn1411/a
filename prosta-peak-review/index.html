What is Prosta Peak and how is it meant to work?

Prosta Peak is a plant-based capsule made for men who want to look after their prostate and urinary comfort as they get older. The prostate often grows with age, and that can bring more trips to the bathroom or a weaker flow. Many men look for supportive nutrition alongside regular check-ups.

Prosta Peak's approach is to combine several traditional plants in a single daily capsule. The best known of them are pygeum, an African tree bark, and saw palmetto, a berry extract that is common in men's prostate formulas. Green tea extract, raspberry, cat's claw and soursop round out the named ingredients. The company describes the mix as a proprietary blend of 20+ plants and nutrients, and recommends taking it for at least 3 to 6 months.

Do prostate herbs like those in Prosta Peak work?

The research is encouraging in places and honest about its limits:

  • A Cochrane review of 18 randomized trials with 1,562 men found that a standardized pygeum extract improved urinary symptoms and flow compared with placebo. Men taking it were more than twice as likely to report overall improvement, and side effects were mild and similar to placebo.[2]
  • Saw palmetto has been tested in many trials. The latest Cochrane update (27 studies, 4,656 men) found it made little difference to symptoms on its own, and also found it probably causes no more side effects than placebo.[3]
  • The pygeum trials were small and short, and the reviewers called for larger, longer studies.[2]

What this means for you: Prosta Peak puts the best-studied prostate herb, pygeum, together with other traditional plants in one easy capsule. The finished formula hasn't had its own clinical trial yet, so give it a few months of daily use alongside your usual check-ups, as the maker suggests, to judge how you feel.

Is Prosta Peak safe? Side effects

Herbal prostate supplements are generally well tolerated by healthy adults, and Prosta Peak contains no stimulants. In the research we reviewed, side effects of pygeum and saw palmetto were mild and similar to placebo. The most common complaint with herbal products in general is mild stomach upset.

  • If you take prescription medicine, have a medical condition, or are allergic to plants, check with your doctor before starting.
  • Dietary supplements aren't reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[1]
  • See a doctor for new or worsening urinary symptoms, such as pain, blood in the urine, a very weak flow or trouble emptying the bladder. These need a professional check, and a supplement is no substitute.
