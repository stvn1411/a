What is The Smoothie Diet and how is it meant to work?

The Smoothie Diet is a digital program rather than a pill or powder. After checkout you download a set of PDF guides that lay out three weeks of meal-replacement smoothies, with the recipes, shopping lists and prep tips you need to follow them.

The author, who presents himself as a certified health coach, explains that the smoothies are given in a specific sequence and frequency, with ingredient and nutrient ratios that change from week to week. You replace certain meals with the smoothies and eat normally for the rest of the day.

The program is built to be flexible: the author says you can keep using it beyond 21 days for as long as you want, using what you learn in the first three weeks.

Do meal-replacement smoothie plans like this work?

The research on meal-replacement plans, the approach behind The Smoothie Diet, is encouraging:

  • A systematic review of 23 trials with 7,884 adults found that weight-loss programs including meal replacements produced greater weight loss at one year than comparison programs, and concluded they are a valid option for managing weight.[1]
  • On average, people using meal replacements lost about 1.4 kg more than people on other diets, and the advantage grew to about 2.2 kg when the meal-replacement plan came with support.[1]
  • The same review notes meal replacements are not a standard recommendation in clinical obesity guidelines, and the trials used meal-replacement products rather than home-made smoothies.[1]

What this means for you: a planned meal-replacement routine is a research-backed way to support weight loss, and The Smoothie Diet puts it in an easy-to-follow, low-cost format. The program itself has not had its own clinical trial, so results depend on how closely you follow it and on your overall eating and activity.

How to get the most out of The Smoothie Diet

  1. Do your week 1 shopping first. The shopping lists are organized by week, so you can start the next day.
  2. Follow the schedule as written. The author designed the order and frequency of the smoothies, and the ratios change week to week.
  3. Prep ahead using the prep guide so smoothies take minutes on busy mornings.
  4. Eat sensibly at the other meals and stay hydrated, so the smoothies are part of a balanced day.
  5. Add movement you enjoy, such as daily walks. Weight-loss research consistently favors programs that include support and activity.
  6. Use the 3-day detox bonus as an optional reset, and the quick-start guide as a printable reference.

Is The Smoothie Diet safe? Side effects

A fruit and vegetable smoothie plan is gentle for most healthy adults, but it changes what and how much you eat, so it is worth a little care.

  • If you have diabetes or another medical condition, take prescription medicine, are pregnant or nursing, or have a history of disordered eating, check with your doctor before starting. The author also says he is not a doctor and that you should consult your physician before beginning a nutrition program.
  • Meal replacements are not a standard recommendation in clinical obesity guidelines, so a healthcare professional can help you decide whether this approach suits you.[1]
  • Stop and speak to a professional if you feel faint, unwell or unusually tired while following the plan.

How The Smoothie Diet compares with other options

  • Other meal-replacement plans: shakes and bars are convenient but often cost much more per month. The Smoothie Diet uses whole-food smoothies and a one-time price.
  • Mediterranean-style or other balanced eating plans: these suit people who want to eat regular meals. Smoothie plans suit people who like quick, simple prep.
  • Working with a dietitian: personalized support is valuable, particularly with medical conditions, and the research suggests plans do better with support.[1]
