What is Yoga for Migraine Relief and how is it meant to work?

Yoga for Migraine Relief is a short, home-based yoga routine from Gentle Stretching. Instead of a long class, you follow a guided video through 10 gentle movements that the program says take 19 minutes or less each day. A PDF manual explains each movement step by step, and a free meditation video is included to help you wind down.

The idea is that slow, controlled movement and relaxation can calm the nervous system, and that calmer, looser muscles in the neck, shoulders and upper body may make life with migraine easier. The program calls its routine "Neuro-Lax" yoga. That is the author's own name for the sequence, and the sales page frames it as a way to complement, not replace, medical care.

It was designed by Rick Kaselj, a kinesiologist with a master's degree in exercise science, and Rebecca Stanley, whose credentials listed on the page include a Yoga Alliance therapeutic yoga certification and a massage therapy diploma. It is delivered by instant download, so you can start the same day.

Does yoga for migraine work?

The research on yoga as an add-on to usual migraine care is encouraging:

  • In the CONTAIN trial, 160 adults with episodic migraine were randomized to medical care alone or medical care plus a 3-month yoga program. Among the 114 who completed it, the yoga group had larger reductions in headache frequency, headache intensity and migraine-related disability, and used fewer pills.[1]
  • In a smaller trial of 60 people, adding 6 weeks of yoga to conventional care improved headache frequency and intensity more than conventional care alone, along with signs of better balance in the autonomic nervous system.[2]
  • The CONTAIN trial was single-centre and open-label, and the yoga group began with a higher headache frequency, so the findings are promising rather than final.[1]

What this means for you: gentle yoga is a research-backed habit to add alongside your usual migraine care, and this program packages it into a short daily routine. It is not a replacement for medical care, and results vary, so give it a few weeks of regular practice and keep your doctor in the loop.

How to get the most out of Yoga for Migraine Relief

  1. Pick a regular time, such as the morning or early evening, so the routine becomes a habit.
  2. Move slowly and stay within comfort. Skip or modify any movement that hurts.
  3. Be consistent. The studies we found ran for 6 to 12 weeks.
  4. Keep a simple headache diary with dates, strength and possible triggers, and share it with your doctor.
  5. Use the meditation video on busy or stressful days to help you wind down.

Is Yoga for Migraine Relief safe? Things to know

Gentle yoga is generally safe for most adults, and the program uses slow, low-impact movements. Still, migraine is a medical condition and deserves proper care.

  • If you have migraines, talk to your doctor before starting, and keep taking any prescribed treatment unless your doctor tells you otherwise.
  • Check with your doctor first if you are pregnant or have neck, back or balance problems.
  • Seek urgent medical care for a sudden, severe headache, or a headache with weakness, confusion, trouble speaking, vision changes or fever.
  • Health and fitness products are not reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness, and the program should be seen as a complement to medical care.
