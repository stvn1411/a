What is Nano-Ease and how is it meant to work?

CBD (cannabidiol) is a compound from the hemp plant that does not make you feel high. It is one of the most talked-about wellness ingredients, but most CBD that you swallow is only partly absorbed. Researchers have found that the amount reaching the bloodstream from oral CBD is low and varies, and that taking it with fats can raise absorption.[2]

Nano-Ease is designed around that problem. The brand mixes broad-spectrum CBD into MCT oil as a "nano mixture" with its NanoZorb technology, and you place a dropperful under your tongue for about 30 seconds. The idea is that the tiny droplets are absorbed through the lining of the mouth. Broad-spectrum means the extract keeps other hemp compounds while the THC is removed, according to the brand.

The sales page tells the story of its founder, Rex Barber, and a "Dr. Tracy" who introduced him to nano CBD. We reviewed the product on its own merits rather than on that story.

Does CBD oil like Nano-Ease work?

CBD is one of the most studied hemp compounds, though the research is still catching up with its popularity:

  • A systematic review of human pharmacokinetic studies found that CBD absorption is higher with food and with lipid-based formulas, which fits Nano-Ease's oil-based design. It also noted the data on absorption is still thin.[2]
  • A meta-analysis of 12 randomized, placebo-controlled trials (803 people) found CBD well tolerated overall, and after excluding childhood epilepsy studies, diarrhea was the only adverse outcome linked to CBD.[3]

What this means for you: Nano-Ease is built on sound ideas, a fat-based carrier and under-the-tongue use, and CBD has a good tolerability record in trials. We did not find a trial of Nano-Ease itself, and the brand's claims about its nano technology come from its own sales page, so see it as a quality CBD routine and give it a few weeks to judge how you feel.

Is Nano-Ease safe? Side effects

In clinical trials CBD has been generally well tolerated by healthy adults, with the most common effects being digestive. Doses in trials are often much higher than in over-the-counter products.[3]

  • In the trials, diarrhea was the main adverse effect, and some liver-test and drowsiness findings appeared in childhood epilepsy studies where people also took other drugs, so check with your doctor if you take prescription medicine.[3]
  • If you are pregnant, nursing or have a liver condition, check with your doctor before using CBD.
  • The brand says the product has no THC. If you are subject to drug testing, ask your employer or doctor first.
  • Dietary supplements aren't reviewed by the FDA for effectiveness before sale, which is true of every supplement.[1]
  • See a doctor for persistent, severe or worsening pain, which needs a proper diagnosis.
