What is the Cooch Ball Intimacy Program and how is it meant to work?

The Cooch Ball Intimacy Program pairs a small, firm blue ball with a six-week guided program. You sit on the ball for a few minutes a day to apply gentle pressure to the pelvic floor, and the program shows you how to combine that with posture, breathing, micro-movements, release work and gentle strengthening exercises.

The founder, Jana, explains on the page that she created it after her own long pain journey, and describes the aim as releasing tension, improving blood flow and rebuilding confidence, rather than simply doing more Kegels. The page reports that over 60,000 balls have been sold; those are the company's own figures.

The six stages are named P.E.L.V.I.C.: Prepare, Energize, Lighten, Vitality, Integrate and Confidence. Week one starts before the ball arrives, so you can begin with posture and breathing straight away.

Does pelvic floor training work?

Pelvic floor muscle training is among the better-researched ways to support sexual function, which gives this program a sound foundation:

  • A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis found that pelvic floor muscle training had positive effects on sexual function in postmenopausal women, especially for orgasm, arousal and satisfaction, though results varied considerably between studies.[1]
  • A meta-analysis of trials in pregnancy and the postpartum period found little effect on sexual function overall, while describing pelvic floor exercises as a safe strategy.[2]

What this means for you: working on the pelvic floor can help, and a structured six-week routine you enjoy is more likely to be kept up than occasional exercises. The Cooch Ball itself has not had a published trial that we could find, so think of it as a convenient tool for the habit, and give it the full six weeks before judging.

How to get the most out of the Cooch Ball Intimacy Program

  1. Start with week one before the ball arrives: the posture and breathing work is part of the plan.
  2. Keep it short and regular. The program is built around about three minutes a day.
  3. Follow the six weeks in order, including the strengthening stage in weeks five and six.
  4. Stop if anything hurts and ask a doctor or pelvic health physiotherapist.
  5. Use the Q&A access if you are unsure about a step.

Is the Cooch Ball safe? Who should check first

Gentle sitting pressure and light exercise are low-risk for most healthy adults, and the page advises consulting a healthcare provider before starting any program.

  • If you have pelvic pain, unexplained bleeding, an infection, a pelvic condition, or recent pelvic surgery, check with your doctor first.
  • If you are pregnant or recently postpartum, ask your doctor or midwife before starting.
  • Stop and see a doctor if you feel sharp pain, numbness or worsening symptoms.
