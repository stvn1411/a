What is Unlock Your Hip Flexors and how is it meant to work?

Unlock Your Hip Flexors is a home program designed for people whose hips feel tight, usually because of long hours of sitting. It was created by kinesiologist and injury specialist Rick Kaselj together with Mike Westerdal of Critical Bench, a long-running strength training site.

The core of the program is a sequential flow of ten exercises. Instead of one stretch held for a long time, the routine moves through different techniques in a set order: PNF stretching, static and dynamic stretching, three-dimensional core stability work, mobility drills, fascia stretching and muscle activation.

You get the routine in two video formats. The coaching video explains each move, the form and how it should feel; the follow-along video lets you do the whole flow without stopping. A manual with photos and descriptions of every move is included, along with two bonuses: a hamstring guide and a 7-day anti-inflammatory diet plan.

Does Unlock Your Hip Flexors work? What the research says

There is no published trial of this specific program, but the basic approach is well supported:

  • In a randomized clinical trial of people with limited hip extension, both passive and active hip flexor stretching improved hip range of motion over six weeks, with neither method clearly better than the other.[1]
  • A systematic review of PNF versus static stretching found that both are effective ways to increase hip-flexion range of motion.[2]
  • In older adults, a supervised hip flexor stretching program improved passive hip extension and was linked to a longer stride in people who started with limited extension.[3]

These studies show that regular stretching and mobility work can increase hip range of motion, which is exactly what this program is built around. The sales page also links tight hips to a long list of other benefits; we focused on the range-of-motion evidence, which is what the research clearly supports.

What this means for you: Unlock Your Hip Flexors turns a well-supported idea, regular and varied hip stretching, into a simple follow-along routine. Do it consistently for a few weeks, move gently and stop if anything hurts; if you have hip or back pain that does not improve, see a physical therapist or doctor.

How to get the most out of Unlock Your Hip Flexors

  1. Watch the coaching video first so you understand the form for each move before using the follow-along version.
  2. Warm up gently with a short walk and move slowly; stretching should feel like a pull, never sharp pain.
  3. Be consistent. Studies on hip flexor stretching measured range of motion gains over roughly 6 to 10 weeks of regular practice.
  4. Break up sitting. Stand and walk for a minute or two every half hour; the program works best alongside less time in a chair.
  5. Build strength too. Squats, bridges and walking keep the hips strong as well as flexible.

Is Unlock Your Hip Flexors safe? Side effects

Gentle stretching and mobility work is safe for most healthy adults, and the program is designed to be done at home without equipment. The main risk is overdoing a stretch.

  • Move slowly and stop if you feel sharp, pinching or radiating pain. Mild stretching tension is normal; pain is not.
  • If you have a hip injury, a hip replacement, recent surgery, a hernia or a diagnosed back condition, check with your doctor or physical therapist first.
  • If you are pregnant or have balance problems, ask your doctor before doing floor-based exercises.
  • The bonus diet guide is general information; if you have a medical condition or food allergy, adapt it with your doctor or dietitian.
  • See a professional for hip, groin or back pain that is severe, persistent or getting worse.

How Unlock Your Hip Flexors compares with other options

  • Free stretching videos: plenty exist, and many are good. This program's advantage is a set order of ten moves, form coaching and a manual in one package, so you don't have to piece a routine together.
  • Yoga or mobility classes: excellent for variety and instruction, but cost more and need set times. A home program fits around your day.
  • Physical therapy: the right choice for pain, injury or a diagnosis, and a good place to start if you are unsure. This program is a low-cost routine for general tightness, not a replacement for treatment.
