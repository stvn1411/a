What is The Lost Book of Remedies and how is it meant to be used?

The Lost Book of Remedies is a printed reference book about medicinal plants and the traditional remedies made from them. It is written by Dr. Nicole Apelian, who the sales page describes as a biologist, herbalist and survival skills instructor with an ethnobotany background, and who appeared on the History Channel show Alone.

Unlike a supplement, you do not take this product. You read it, use it as a field guide, and decide for yourself what to try. Each plant has between two and four colour photographs and identification notes, followed by traditional uses and how to prepare them as teas, tinctures, salves, syrups, poultices and similar forms.

The book covers a wide range of everyday topics, from plants for tea and cooking to home-made syrups and garden growing. It is a pleasant mix of nature guide, kitchen reference and history of folk traditions.

Does a book like this deliver real value?

A book is judged differently from a supplement, so here is how we looked at it:

  • Identification and preparation: clear photos, two ways to search and step-by-step recipes are what make an herbal guide usable, and this book is built around them.
  • Herbal traditions: many plants in the book have a long history of traditional use. Traditional use is not the same as proof of benefit, and the sales page does not present clinical trial data for its remedies.
  • Safety guidance: the US National Institutes of Health reminds people that some herbs can interact with medicine and that anyone using a complementary approach should tell their health care providers.[2][3]

Reader comments on the page are mostly enthusiastic about the photos and ease of reading, and one reviewer suggested the layout could be organised better. That mix reads as honest to us.

What this means for you: the book is a wonderful way to learn about the plants around you and about herbal traditions. Treat remedies as folk knowledge to enjoy and explore, check with your doctor about anything you plan to take, and keep seeing a professional for health concerns.

How to get the most out of the book

  1. Start with the plants near you. Use the plant index to find what grows in your garden or neighbourhood.
  2. Learn identification first. Match photos carefully and never use a plant you cannot identify with confidence.
  3. Try gentle recipes first, such as teas and culinary uses, before anything more involved.
  4. Use the bonuses: the garden guide and videos are good starting points.
  5. Check with your doctor before using any remedy, especially if you take medicine or have a health condition.

Is the book safe to use? Safety notes

A book is safe to read, and the main safety questions are about what you choose to try. Plants are real medicines with real effects, so care matters.

  • If you take prescription medicine, ask your doctor or pharmacist before trying any herb, because herbs can interact with medicines.[2]
  • If you are pregnant or nursing, or planning to give a remedy to a child, check with a health professional first.
  • Never eat or use a wild plant unless you have identified it with certainty.
  • Do not use plant remedies in place of professional care for serious, new or worsening symptoms.[3]
  • Information in the book is general, and statements about remedies have not been evaluated by the FDA.[1]
